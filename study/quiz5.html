Quiz 5 practice problems

From: Quiz 5 Practice Problems + Answer Key

Recent quiz problems covering Fall 26 Quiz 5 material from Weeks 10 and 11. The answer key only gives final answers, so every solution below is worked out in full and checked against that key.

The five formulas this whole quiz uses

  • Arc length of \(y=f(x)\) on \([a,b]\): \[L=\int_a^b\sqrt{1+(f'(x))^2}\,dx\]
  • Surface area when you spin a curve: \[SA=\int 2\pi\,(\text{radius})\,ds\] Spinning about the x-axis the radius is \(y\). Spinning about the y-axis the radius is \(x\). Here \(ds=\sqrt{1+(y')^2}\,dx\) (or \(\sqrt{1+(x')^2}\,dy\) when the curve is given as \(x=g(y)\)).
  • Average value: \[f_{avg}=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\]
  • Centroid of the region between \(y=f(x)\) (top) and \(y=g(x)\) (bottom): \[A=\int_a^b (f-g)\,dx,\quad \bar x=\frac{1}{A}\int_a^b x\,(f-g)\,dx,\quad \bar y=\frac{1}{A}\cdot\frac12\int_a^b (f^2-g^2)\,dx\] With a density \(\rho(x)\), put \(\rho(x)\) inside every integral, including the one for mass.
  • Probability density: total area is 1, \(P(a\le X\le b)=\int_a^b f\), mean \(=\int x f\,dx\), variance \(=\int x^2f\,dx-(\text{mean})^2\), median \(m\) solves \(\int_{\text{start}}^m f\,dx=\tfrac12\).

Arc length and surface area (problems 1 to 9)

1Arc length of a curve with fractional powers

Let \(f(x)=\tfrac23x^{3/2}-\tfrac12x^{1/2}\). Find the arc length over \(\tfrac14\le x\le 4\).
Solution
Plan. Differentiate, square, add 1, and hope it becomes a perfect square. It does. Then the square root disappears.
  1. Differentiate (power rule on each term): \[f'(x)=x^{1/2}-\tfrac14x^{-1/2}\]
  2. Square it. Use \((A-B)^2=A^2-2AB+B^2\) with \(A=x^{1/2}\), \(B=\tfrac14x^{-1/2}\). The middle term is \(2AB=2\cdot x^{1/2}\cdot\tfrac14x^{-1/2}=\tfrac12\). \[(f')^2=x-\tfrac12+\tfrac1{16}x^{-1}\]
  3. Add 1. The \(-\tfrac12\) becomes \(+\tfrac12\), which is exactly the middle term of \((A+B)^2\): \[1+(f')^2=x+\tfrac12+\tfrac1{16}x^{-1}=\left(x^{1/2}+\tfrac14x^{-1/2}\right)^2\]
  4. The square root cancels the square: \[L=\int_{1/4}^{4}\left(x^{1/2}+\tfrac14x^{-1/2}\right)dx\]
  5. Integrate each term (raise the power by 1, divide by the new power): \[\left[\tfrac23x^{3/2}+\tfrac12x^{1/2}\right]_{1/4}^{4}\]
  6. Plug in \(x=4\): \(\tfrac23\cdot8+\tfrac12\cdot2=\tfrac{16}{3}+1=\tfrac{19}{3}\). Plug in \(x=\tfrac14\): \(\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac18+\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12=\tfrac1{12}+\tfrac14=\tfrac13\).
  7. Subtract: \(\tfrac{19}{3}-\tfrac13=6\).
Answer. \(L=6\)
Watch out. when you square \(A-B\) do not forget the middle term. If \(1+(f')^2\) is not a perfect square, recheck your derivative first.

2Surface area, parabola spun about the y-axis

Find the surface area formed by rotating \(y=1-x^2\) for \(0\le x\le\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\) about the y-axis.
Solution
Plan. Spinning about the y-axis means radius \(=x\). The curve is given as \(y=f(x)\), so integrate in \(x\).
  1. Slope: \(y'=-2x\), so \(ds=\sqrt{1+4x^2}\,dx\).
  2. Set up: \[SA=2\pi\int_0^{\sqrt3/2}x\sqrt{1+4x^2}\,dx\]
  3. Substitute \(u=1+4x^2\), so \(du=8x\,dx\) and \(x\,dx=\tfrac18du\).
  4. New limits: \(x=0\to u=1\). \(x=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\to u=1+4\cdot\tfrac34=4\).
  5. Integrate: \[2\pi\cdot\tfrac18\int_1^4u^{1/2}du=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\cdot\tfrac23\left[u^{3/2}\right]_1^4=\tfrac{\pi}{6}(8-1)\]
Answer. \(SA=\dfrac{7\pi}{6}\)
Watch out. change the limits when you substitute, or convert back to \(x\) before plugging in. Do not mix them.

3Surface area, curve given as x in terms of y

Find the surface area generated by revolving \(x=\tfrac13(y^2+2)^{3/2}\), \(1\le y\le2\), about the x-axis.
Solution
Plan. Spinning about the x-axis means radius \(=y\). The curve is \(x=g(y)\), so integrate in \(y\) and use \(ds=\sqrt{1+(x')^2}\,dy\).
  1. Differentiate with the chain rule: \(x'=\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac32(y^2+2)^{1/2}\cdot2y=y\sqrt{y^2+2}\).
  2. Square and add 1: \(1+y^2(y^2+2)=y^4+2y^2+1=(y^2+1)^2\).
  3. So \(ds=(y^2+1)\,dy\). The radical is gone.
  4. Set up: \[SA=2\pi\int_1^2 y\,(y^2+1)\,dy=2\pi\int_1^2(y^3+y)\,dy\]
  5. Integrate: \[2\pi\left[\tfrac{y^4}{4}+\tfrac{y^2}{2}\right]_1^2=2\pi\left[(4+2)-\left(\tfrac14+\tfrac12\right)\right]=2\pi\cdot\tfrac{21}{4}\]
Answer. \(SA=\dfrac{21\pi}{2}\)

4Surface area of a piece of a circle

Find the surface area generated by revolving \(y=\sqrt{25-x^2}\) on \([3,4]\) about the x-axis.
Solution
Plan. Radius \(=y\). The messy parts cancel. This is a good one to trust the algebra on.
  1. Derivative: \(y'=\dfrac{-x}{\sqrt{25-x^2}}\).
  2. \(1+(y')^2=1+\dfrac{x^2}{25-x^2}=\dfrac{25}{25-x^2}\).
  3. So \(ds=\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{25-x^2}}\,dx\).
  4. Multiply radius times \(ds\): \(\sqrt{25-x^2}\cdot\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{25-x^2}}=5\).
  5. \[SA=2\pi\int_3^4 5\,dx=10\pi\,(4-3)\]
Answer. \(SA=10\pi\)

5Exact arc length, perfect square trick

Find the exact arc length of \(y=\dfrac{x^4}{16}+\dfrac{1}{2x^2}\) on \([1,2]\).
Solution
Plan. Rewrite with exponents, differentiate, square, add 1, recognize the perfect square.
  1. Rewrite: \(y=\tfrac1{16}x^4+\tfrac12x^{-2}\).
  2. Differentiate: \(y'=\tfrac{x^3}{4}-x^{-3}\).
  3. Square (with \(A=\tfrac{x^3}{4}\), \(B=x^{-3}\), \(2AB=\tfrac12\)): \((y')^2=\tfrac{x^6}{16}-\tfrac12+x^{-6}\).
  4. Add 1: \(1+(y')^2=\tfrac{x^6}{16}+\tfrac12+x^{-6}=\left(\tfrac{x^3}{4}+x^{-3}\right)^2\).
  5. \[L=\int_1^2\left(\tfrac{x^3}{4}+x^{-3}\right)dx=\left[\tfrac{x^4}{16}-\tfrac{1}{2x^2}\right]_1^2\]
  6. At 2: \(1-\tfrac18=\tfrac78\). At 1: \(\tfrac1{16}-\tfrac12=-\tfrac7{16}\).
  7. Subtract: \(\tfrac78+\tfrac7{16}=\tfrac{14}{16}+\tfrac7{16}=\tfrac{21}{16}\).
Answer. \(L=\dfrac{21}{16}\)
Watch out. \(\int x^{-3}dx=\dfrac{x^{-2}}{-2}\). The minus sign comes from dividing by \(-2\).

6Arc length, power of a polynomial

Find the arc length of \(y=\tfrac23\left(4x^2+\tfrac12\right)^{3/2}\) for \(0\le x\le3\).
Solution
Plan. Chain rule, then watch the square root disappear.
  1. \(y'=\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac32\left(4x^2+\tfrac12\right)^{1/2}\cdot8x=8x\sqrt{4x^2+\tfrac12}\).
  2. Square: \((y')^2=64x^2\left(4x^2+\tfrac12\right)=256x^4+32x^2\).
  3. Add 1: \(256x^4+32x^2+1=(16x^2+1)^2\). Check: \((16x^2)^2=256x^4\), \(2\cdot16x^2\cdot1=32x^2\), \(1^2=1\).
  4. \[L=\int_0^3(16x^2+1)\,dx=\left[\tfrac{16}{3}x^3+x\right]_0^3=\tfrac{16}{3}\cdot27+3=144+3\]
Answer. \(L=147\)

7Arc length of a parametric curve

Compute the arc length of \(x=\tfrac13(2t-1)^{3/2}\), \(y=\tfrac12(t-1)^2\), for \(1\le t\le3\).
Solution
Plan. For \(x(t),y(t)\) the arc length element is \(ds=\sqrt{(x')^2+(y')^2}\,dt\).
  1. \(x'=\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac32(2t-1)^{1/2}\cdot2=\sqrt{2t-1}\).
  2. \(y'=t-1\).
  3. \((x')^2+(y')^2=(2t-1)+(t^2-2t+1)=t^2\).
  4. \(\sqrt{t^2}=t\) (since \(t>0\)).
  5. \[L=\int_1^3t\,dt=\tfrac12\left(9-1\right)\]
Answer. \(L=4\)

8Surface area, x = y cubed, spun about the y-axis

Find the surface area generated by rotating \(x=y^3\) for \(0\le y\le\left(\tfrac13\right)^{1/4}\) about the y-axis.
Solution
Plan. Spinning about the y-axis means radius \(=x\). Since the curve is \(x=g(y)\), write the radius as \(y^3\) and integrate in \(y\).
  1. \(x'=3y^2\), so \(ds=\sqrt{1+9y^4}\,dy\).
  2. \[SA=2\pi\int y^3\sqrt{1+9y^4}\,dy\]
  3. Substitute \(u=1+9y^4\), so \(du=36y^3dy\) and \(y^3dy=\tfrac1{36}du\).
  4. Limits: \(y=0\to u=1\). \(y^4=\tfrac13\to u=1+3=4\).
  5. \[2\pi\cdot\tfrac1{36}\int_1^4u^{1/2}du=\tfrac{\pi}{18}\cdot\tfrac23\left[u^{3/2}\right]_1^4=\tfrac{\pi}{27}(8-1)\]
Answer. \(SA=\dfrac{7\pi}{27}\)

9Surface area of a square root curve

Find the surface area generated by revolving \(y=\sqrt x\) about the x-axis for \(0\le x\le2\).
Solution
Plan. Radius \(=y=\sqrt x\). The \(\sqrt x\) cancels against the \(ds\).
  1. \(y'=\dfrac1{2\sqrt x}\). Then \(1+(y')^2=1+\dfrac1{4x}=\dfrac{4x+1}{4x}\).
  2. \(ds=\dfrac{\sqrt{4x+1}}{2\sqrt x}\,dx\).
  3. Radius times \(ds\): \(\sqrt x\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{4x+1}}{2\sqrt x}=\dfrac{\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\).
  4. \[SA=2\pi\int_0^2\tfrac12\sqrt{4x+1}\,dx=\pi\int_0^2\sqrt{4x+1}\,dx\]
  5. Substitute \(u=4x+1\), \(dx=\tfrac14du\). Limits: \(1\) to \(9\).
  6. \[\pi\cdot\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac23\left[u^{3/2}\right]_1^9=\tfrac{\pi}{6}(27-1)=\tfrac{26\pi}{6}\]
Answer. \(SA=\dfrac{13\pi}{3}\)

Average value (problems 10 and 11)

10Average value with a trig substitution

Find the average value of \(f(x)=\dfrac{16}{(x^2+4)^{3/2}}\) on \([0,2\sqrt3]\).
Solution
Plan. Average value is the integral divided by the interval length. The integral needs the substitution \(x=2\tan\theta\), because \(x^2+4\) is a square plus a number.
  1. Substitute \(x=2\tan\theta\). Then \(dx=2\sec^2\theta\,d\theta\) and \(x^2+4=4\tan^2\theta+4=4\sec^2\theta\).
  2. So \((x^2+4)^{3/2}=(4\sec^2\theta)^{3/2}=8\sec^3\theta\).
  3. The integrand becomes \[\frac{16\cdot2\sec^2\theta}{8\sec^3\theta}\,d\theta=\frac{4}{\sec\theta}\,d\theta=4\cos\theta\,d\theta\]
  4. New limits: \(x=0\to\tan\theta=0\to\theta=0\). \(x=2\sqrt3\to\tan\theta=\sqrt3\to\theta=\tfrac\pi3\).
  5. \[\int_0^{\pi/3}4\cos\theta\,d\theta=4\sin\theta\Big|_0^{\pi/3}=4\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=2\sqrt3\]
  6. Divide by the length of the interval, \(2\sqrt3-0=2\sqrt3\): \(f_{avg}=\dfrac{2\sqrt3}{2\sqrt3}=1\).
Answer. \(f_{avg}=1\)

11Average value with partial fractions

Find the average value of \(f(x)=\dfrac{6}{x^2-1}\) on \([2,5]\).
Solution
Plan. Factor the denominator and split into two simple fractions (partial fractions).
  1. \(x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)\). Write \(\dfrac{6}{(x-1)(x+1)}=\dfrac{3}{x-1}-\dfrac{3}{x+1}\). Check: \(3(x+1)-3(x-1)=6\).
  2. Integrate: \(\int\left(\dfrac3{x-1}-\dfrac3{x+1}\right)dx=3\ln|x-1|-3\ln|x+1|\).
  3. Plug in 5: \(3\ln4-3\ln6=3\ln\tfrac23\). Plug in 2: \(3\ln1-3\ln3=-3\ln3\).
  4. Subtract: \(3\ln\tfrac23+3\ln3=3\ln2\).
  5. Divide by \(5-2=3\): \(f_{avg}=\ln2\).
Answer. \(f_{avg}=\ln 2\)

Centroids and center of mass (problems 12 to 17)

12y-coordinate of a centroid between sine and cosine

Find the y-coordinate of the centroid of the region bounded by \(y=\cos x\) and \(y=\sin x\) for \(\tfrac\pi4\le x\le\tfrac{3\pi}4\).
Solution
Plan. On this interval \(\sin x\) is on top. You need the area \(A\) and the moment \(M_x=\tfrac12\int(f^2-g^2)\,dx\), then \(\bar y=M_x/A\).
  1. Area: \[A=\int_{\pi/4}^{3\pi/4}(\sin x-\cos x)\,dx=\left[-\cos x-\sin x\right]_{\pi/4}^{3\pi/4}\] At \(\tfrac{3\pi}4\): \(\tfrac{\sqrt2}2-\tfrac{\sqrt2}2=0\). At \(\tfrac\pi4\): \(-\tfrac{\sqrt2}2-\tfrac{\sqrt2}2=-\sqrt2\). So \(A=0-(-\sqrt2)=\sqrt2\).
  2. Moment: \(\sin^2x-\cos^2x=-\cos2x\), so \[M_x=\tfrac12\int_{\pi/4}^{3\pi/4}(-\cos2x)\,dx=-\tfrac14\sin2x\Big|_{\pi/4}^{3\pi/4}\] \(\sin\tfrac{3\pi}2=-1\) and \(\sin\tfrac\pi2=1\), so \(M_x=-\tfrac14(-1-1)=\tfrac12\).
  3. \(\bar y=\dfrac{1/2}{\sqrt2}=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{4}\).
Answer. \(\bar y=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{4}\)
Watch out. \(\sin^2x-\cos^2x=-\cos 2x\) is the identity doing the work here.

13x-coordinate of a triangle's centroid

Find the x-coordinate of the centroid of the triangle with vertices \((0,0)\), \((0,4)\), \((6,0)\).
Solution
Plan. The centroid of any triangle is the average of its three vertices. No integral needed.
  1. \(\bar x=\dfrac{0+0+6}{3}=2\).
  2. Sanity check with the right-triangle formula from the notes: legs \(b=6\) and \(h=4\) give \((\tfrac b3,\tfrac h3)=(2,\tfrac43)\).
Answer. \(\bar x=2\)

14Center of mass with density

The region is bounded by \(y=\sqrt x\) and \(y=x^2\), with density \(\rho(x)=35x\). Find \(\bar x\).
Solution
Plan. With a density that only depends on \(x\), each vertical strip has mass \(\rho(x)\,(f-g)\,dx\). Then \(\bar x=\dfrac{\int x\,\rho\,(f-g)}{\int\rho\,(f-g)}\).
  1. The curves meet where \(\sqrt x=x^2\), at \(x=0\) and \(x=1\). On \([0,1]\), \(\sqrt x\) is on top.
  2. Mass: \[m=\int_0^1 35x\left(x^{1/2}-x^2\right)dx=35\int_0^1\left(x^{3/2}-x^3\right)dx=35\left[\tfrac25x^{5/2}-\tfrac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^1=35\left(\tfrac25-\tfrac14\right)=35\cdot\tfrac3{20}=\tfrac{21}4\]
  3. Moment about the y-axis: \[M_y=\int_0^1 x\cdot35x\left(x^{1/2}-x^2\right)dx=35\int_0^1\left(x^{5/2}-x^4\right)dx=35\left[\tfrac27x^{7/2}-\tfrac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^1=35\left(\tfrac27-\tfrac15\right)=35\cdot\tfrac{3}{35}=3\]
  4. \(\bar x=\dfrac{3}{21/4}=\dfrac{12}{21}=\dfrac47\).
Answer. \(\bar x=\dfrac47\)
Watch out. the density goes inside both integrals. Forgetting it in the mass integral is the usual slip.

15Centroid between two parabolas

Find the centroid of the region bounded by \(y=2x^2\) and \(y=3-x^2\).
Solution
Plan. Find where they cross, use symmetry for \(\bar x\), then compute \(A\) and \(M_x\).
  1. Intersection: \(2x^2=3-x^2\Rightarrow3x^2=3\Rightarrow x=\pm1\). The top curve is \(3-x^2\).
  2. The region is symmetric about the y-axis, so \(\bar x=0\).
  3. Area: \[A=\int_{-1}^1(3-3x^2)\,dx=2\left[3x-x^3\right]_0^1=2\cdot2=4\]
  4. Moment: \(f^2-g^2=(3-x^2)^2-(2x^2)^2=9-6x^2+x^4-4x^4=9-6x^2-3x^4\). \[M_x=\tfrac12\int_{-1}^1(9-6x^2-3x^4)\,dx=\int_0^1(9-6x^2-3x^4)\,dx=\left[9x-2x^3-\tfrac35x^5\right]_0^1=7-\tfrac35=\tfrac{32}5\]
  5. \(\bar y=\dfrac{32/5}{4}=\dfrac85\).
Answer. \((\bar x,\bar y)=\left(0,\dfrac85\right)\)

16Centroid of a triangle plus a quarter circle

Region bounded below by the x-axis, on the left by the line \(y=x+2\), and on the right by the quarter circle of radius 2 (shaded in the graph).
Solution
Plan. Do not integrate. Split the region into two shapes whose centroids you know, then take the area-weighted average of their centroids.
  1. The line \(y=x+2\) runs from \((-2,0)\) up to \((0,2)\). So the left piece is a triangle with vertices \((-2,0)\), \((0,0)\), \((0,2)\). Area \(A_1=\tfrac12\cdot2\cdot2=2\). Centroid: average of vertices \(=\left(-\tfrac23,\tfrac23\right)\).
  2. The right piece is a quarter circle of radius 2 in the first quadrant. Area \(A_2=\tfrac14\pi\cdot2^2=\pi\). Its centroid is \(\left(\tfrac{4r}{3\pi},\tfrac{4r}{3\pi}\right)=\left(\tfrac8{3\pi},\tfrac8{3\pi}\right)\).
  3. Total area: \(2+\pi\).
  4. \[\bar x=\frac{2\left(-\tfrac23\right)+\pi\cdot\tfrac{8}{3\pi}}{\pi+2}=\frac{-\tfrac43+\tfrac83}{\pi+2}=\frac{4}{3(\pi+2)}\]
  5. \[\bar y=\frac{2\cdot\tfrac23+\pi\cdot\tfrac{8}{3\pi}}{\pi+2}=\frac{\tfrac43+\tfrac83}{\pi+2}=\frac{4}{\pi+2}\]
Answer. \((\bar x,\bar y)=\left(\dfrac{4}{3(\pi+2)},\ \dfrac{4}{\pi+2}\right)\approx(0.259,\ 0.778)\)
Watch out. the triangle is on the left of the y-axis, so its x-coordinate is negative. The \(\pi\) in the quarter circle's area cancels the \(\pi\) in its centroid.

17ȳ with density, quarter circle region

The region in the first quadrant is bounded by \(y=\sqrt{4-x^2}\), the x-axis and the y-axis. The density is \(\rho(x)=x\). Find \(\bar y\).
Solution
Plan. Each vertical strip has mass \(\rho(x)f(x)\,dx\) and its own center sits at height \(\tfrac f2\). So \(M_x=\int\rho\cdot\tfrac12f^2\,dx\) and \(\bar y=M_x/m\).
  1. Mass: \[m=\int_0^2x\sqrt{4-x^2}\,dx\] Substitute \(u=4-x^2\), \(du=-2x\,dx\): \(m=-\tfrac12\int_4^0u^{1/2}du=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac23\left[u^{3/2}\right]_0^4=\tfrac13\cdot8=\tfrac83\).
  2. Moment about the x-axis: \[M_x=\int_0^2x\cdot\tfrac12(4-x^2)\,dx=\tfrac12\left[2x^2-\tfrac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^2=\tfrac12(8-4)=2\]
  3. \(\bar y=\dfrac{2}{8/3}=\dfrac34\).
Answer. \(\bar y=\dfrac34\)

Probability densities, mean, median, standard deviation (problems 18 to 25)

18Probability from a density (answer of the form √a over b)

\(f(x)=\dfrac{75/4}{(25-x^2)^{3/2}}\) for \(0\le x\le4\) (zero otherwise) is a probability density. Find \(P\left(X\le\tfrac52\right)\). The answer is \(\dfrac{\sqrt a}{b}\); enter \(a\) and \(b\).
Solution
Plan. Integrate \(f\) from 0 to \(\tfrac52\). The integral needs \(x=5\sin\theta\) (because of \(25-x^2\)).
  1. Substitute \(x=5\sin\theta\). Then \(dx=5\cos\theta\,d\theta\) and \(25-x^2=25\cos^2\theta\), so \((25-x^2)^{3/2}=125\cos^3\theta\).
  2. \[\int\frac{dx}{(25-x^2)^{3/2}}=\int\frac{5\cos\theta}{125\cos^3\theta}\,d\theta=\tfrac1{25}\int\sec^2\theta\,d\theta=\tfrac1{25}\tan\theta\] and \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{25-x^2}}\), so the antiderivative is \(\dfrac{x}{25\sqrt{25-x^2}}\).
  3. Multiply by \(\tfrac{75}{4}\): the antiderivative of \(f\) is \(F(x)=\tfrac34\cdot\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{25-x^2}}\).
  4. Check the total: \(F(4)=\tfrac34\cdot\tfrac43=1\). Good, that is why the constant is \(\tfrac{75}4\).
  5. \(P(X\le\tfrac52)=F(\tfrac52)-F(0)=\tfrac34\cdot\dfrac{5/2}{\sqrt{25-25/4}}=\tfrac34\cdot\dfrac{5/2}{\tfrac{5\sqrt3}{2}}=\tfrac34\cdot\dfrac1{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\).
Answer. \(P=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\), so \(a=3\) and \(b=4\).

19Find the constant C

\(f(x)=Cxe^{-x/2}\) for \(x\ge0\) (zero for \(x<0\)). Find \(C\) so that \(f\) is a probability density.
Solution
Plan. The total area must be 1. Integrate by parts.
  1. Need \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty xe^{-x/2}dx\). Integration by parts: \(u=x\), \(dv=e^{-x/2}dx\), so \(du=dx\), \(v=-2e^{-x/2}\).
  2. \[\int xe^{-x/2}dx=-2xe^{-x/2}+2\int e^{-x/2}dx=-2xe^{-x/2}-4e^{-x/2}\]
  3. From 0 to \(\infty\): the upper limit goes to 0 (exponentials beat \(x\)). The lower limit at \(0\) gives \(-4\). So the integral is \(0-(-4)=4\).
  4. Set \(C\cdot4=1\).
Answer. \(C=\dfrac14\)

20Same density, strict inequality

Same \(f\) as problem 18. Find \(P\left(X<\tfrac52\right)\).
Solution
Plan. Same integral. For a continuous density, "less than" and "less than or equal" give the same probability, because a single point has zero area.
  1. Use \(F(x)=\tfrac34\cdot\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{25-x^2}}\) from problem 18.
  2. \(F\left(\tfrac52\right)-F(0)=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\).
Answer. \(P=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\approx0.433\)

21Median

\(g(x)=3\sin(3x)\) for \(0\le x\le\tfrac\pi6\) is a density. Find the median.
Solution
Plan. The median \(m\) is the point where half the area is on each side: \(\int_0^mg=\tfrac12\).
  1. \(\displaystyle\int_0^m3\sin3x\,dx=\left[-\cos3x\right]_0^m=1-\cos3m\).
  2. Set equal to \(\tfrac12\): \(\cos3m=\tfrac12\).
  3. \(3m=\tfrac\pi3\) (the angle in \([0,\tfrac\pi2]\) whose cosine is \(\tfrac12\)). So \(m=\tfrac\pi9\).
Answer. median \(=\dfrac\pi9\)
Watch out. the angle is \(3m\), not \(m\). Divide by 3 at the end.

22Mean

Same \(g\). Find the mean.
Solution
Plan. Mean \(=\int x\,g(x)\,dx\). Integration by parts.
  1. \(\displaystyle\mu=\int_0^{\pi/6}x\cdot3\sin3x\,dx\). Let \(u=x\), \(dv=3\sin3x\,dx\), so \(v=-\cos3x\).
  2. \[\mu=\left[-x\cos3x\right]_0^{\pi/6}+\int_0^{\pi/6}\cos3x\,dx\]
  3. First part: at \(\tfrac\pi6\), \(\cos\tfrac\pi2=0\); at \(0\), it is \(0\). So it is \(0\).
  4. Second part: \(\left[\tfrac13\sin3x\right]_0^{\pi/6}=\tfrac13(1-0)=\tfrac13\).
Answer. mean \(=\dfrac13\)

23Standard deviation

\(g(x)=\dfrac3{x^4}\) for \(x\ge1\) is a density. Find the standard deviation.
Solution
Plan. Standard deviation \(=\sqrt{\text{variance}}\) and variance \(=E[X^2]-(E[X])^2\). Compute both \(E[X]\) and \(E[X^2]\).
  1. \(E[X]=\displaystyle\int_1^\infty x\cdot3x^{-4}dx=3\int_1^\infty x^{-3}dx=3\left[-\tfrac{1}{2x^2}\right]_1^\infty=\tfrac32\).
  2. \(E[X^2]=\displaystyle\int_1^\infty x^2\cdot3x^{-4}dx=3\int_1^\infty x^{-2}dx=3\left[-\tfrac1x\right]_1^\infty=3\).
  3. Variance: \(3-\left(\tfrac32\right)^2=3-\tfrac94=\tfrac34\).
  4. \(\sigma=\sqrt{\tfrac34}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\).
Answer. \(\sigma=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\)

24Median of an exponential density

\(f(x)=\tfrac13e^{-x/3}\) for \(x\ge0\). Find the median.
Solution
Plan. Same idea as problem 21: solve \(\int_0^mf=\tfrac12\).
  1. \(\displaystyle\int_0^m\tfrac13e^{-x/3}dx=\left[-e^{-x/3}\right]_0^m=1-e^{-m/3}\).
  2. Set equal to \(\tfrac12\): \(e^{-m/3}=\tfrac12\).
  3. Take \(\ln\) of both sides: \(-\tfrac m3=\ln\tfrac12=-\ln2\).
  4. \(m=3\ln2\).
Answer. median \(=3\ln2\approx2.079\)

25Mean with a logarithm

\(f(x)=\dfrac{2\ln x}{x}\) for \(1\le x\le e\). Find the mean.
Solution
Plan. Mean \(=\int xf(x)\,dx\). The \(x\) cancels the \(\tfrac1x\).
  1. \(\displaystyle\mu=\int_1^ex\cdot\frac{2\ln x}{x}\,dx=2\int_1^e\ln x\,dx\).
  2. \(\int\ln x\,dx=x\ln x-x\) (this one is worth memorizing).
  3. \(2\left[x\ln x-x\right]_1^e=2\left[(e-e)-(0-1)\right]=2\cdot1\).
Answer. mean \(=2\)
