Lectures 42 and 43

From: Practice Problems + Solutions

Lectures 42 and 43: probability with areas and with density functions. Several problems the PDF leaves unsolved (7 is solved there, 10 and 12 are not) are worked out here.

What to remember

  • Geometric probability. If a point is chosen uniformly at random in a region, then \[P(\text{point lands in a part})=\frac{\text{size of the part}}{\text{size of the whole}}\] where "size" is length, area, or volume, whatever fits the problem. Draw the whole region, shade the part you want, find both areas.
  • Probability density function (pdf). A function \(f\) is a pdf on its domain if (1) \(f(x)\ge0\) everywhere, and (2) the total area under it is 1: \(\int f\,dx=1\).
  • Probabilities from a pdf. \(P(a\le X\le b)=\int_a^bf(x)\,dx\). For a continuous variable, \(P(X=a)=0\), so "\(<\)" and "\(\le\)" give the same answer.
  • Fixing a pdf. If \(f\) almost works, multiply by a constant \(C\) chosen so the total area is 1: \(C=\dfrac{1}{\int f\,dx}\). Or solve for an unknown endpoint.
  • Exponential distribution. \(f(x)=\lambda e^{-\lambda x}\) for \(x\ge0\). Total area: \(\int_0^\infty\lambda e^{-\lambda x}dx=1\). \(P(X<c)=1-e^{-\lambda c}\) and \(P(X>c)=e^{-\lambda c}\).
  • Improper integral habit. An integral to \(\infty\) means a limit: \(\int_k^\infty=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_k^t\). Terms like \(e^{-t}\) go to 0.

Geometric probability (problems 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 11)

1Region between a parabola and a line inside a rectangle

\(A\) is the region bounded by \(x=y^2\) and \(x=9\). \(R\) is the rectangle with vertices \((0,-4),(0,4),(10,4),(10,-4)\). Find the probability that a random point in \(R\) lies in \(A\).
Solution
  1. \(P=\dfrac{\text{Area}(A)}{\text{Area}(R)}\).
  2. Area of \(R\): width \(10\), height \(8\), so \(80\).
  3. Area of \(A\): the curve \(x=y^2\) meets \(x=9\) at \(y=\pm3\). Integrate in \(y\) (right edge minus left edge): \[\int_{-3}^3\left(9-y^2\right)dy=\left[9y-\tfrac{y^3}3\right]_{-3}^3=(27-9)-(-27+9)=36\]
  4. \(P=\dfrac{36}{80}\).
Answer. \(P=\dfrac{9}{20}=0.45\), which is \(45\%\). (The PDF writes "0.45%"; that percent sign is a typo. \(0.45\) already means \(45\%\).)

4A drop landing outside a circle in a bucket

Water drips uniformly at random into a circular bucket 10 inches across. What is the probability a drop lands more than 3 inches from the center?
Solution
  1. Diameter 10 means radius 5. Whole region: area \(\pi\cdot5^2=25\pi\).
  2. "More than 3 inches from center" is the ring outside the radius-3 circle. Its area is \(25\pi-9\pi=16\pi\).
Answer. \(P=\dfrac{16\pi}{25\pi}=\dfrac{16}{25}\)
Watch out. the \(\pi\) cancels. If you still have a \(\pi\) at the end, recheck.

5Bait drifting less than 5 degrees

A bait is cast from the center of a circular lake and ends up at a uniformly random position. What is the probability it is within 5 degrees of its starting direction?
Solution
  1. "Less than 5 degrees on either side" is a sector with total angle \(10^\circ\).
  2. \(P=\dfrac{\text{sector area}}{\text{circle area}}=\dfrac{10^\circ}{360^\circ}\). (Equivalently \(\dfrac{\tfrac12\theta r^2}{\pi r^2}\) with \(\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{18}\).)
Answer. \(P=\dfrac{1}{36}\)

6Baseball hitting the high part of a barn

The barn face is a right triangle. The hypotenuse is the 20-foot base and the height is 10 feet. A baseball hits a uniformly random spot. What is the probability it hits higher than 7 feet?
Solution
  1. The base is 20 and the height is 10, so total area \(=\tfrac12\cdot20\cdot10=100\).
  2. Put the apex at \((0,10)\) and the base from \(x=-10\) to \(x=10\). The right side is the line \(y=-x+10\).
  3. At height \(y=7\): \(7=-x+10\), so \(x=3\). The part above \(y=7\) is a small triangle of base \(6\) (from \(x=-3\) to \(3\)) and height \(3\).
  4. Its area: \(\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot3=9\).
Answer. \(P=\dfrac{9}{100}\)

7Football through the goalposts

The posts are a rectangle 10 feet wide and 8 feet high. A football passes through at a uniformly random point. Find the probability that \(y\ge\tfrac45x+4\).
Solution
  1. Whole region: \(0\le x\le10\), \(0\le y\le8\). Area \(80\).
  2. The line \(y=\tfrac45x+4\) starts at height \(4\) when \(x=0\) and reaches \(y=8\) when \(\tfrac45x=4\), so \(x=5\).
  3. The region above the line inside the rectangle is a triangle with height \(8-4=4\) and base \(5\). Its area is \(\tfrac12\cdot4\cdot5=10\).
Answer. \(P=\dfrac{10}{80}=\dfrac18\)

11Oil drop landing in a rectangle near the center

Oil drips uniformly into a circular pan of radius 14 inches. What is the probability it lands no more than 4 inches horizontally and no more than 2 inches vertically from the center?
Solution
  1. The target is the rectangle \(-4\le x\le4\), \(-2\le y\le2\). Its area is \(8\cdot4=32\).
  2. (It fits in the pan: its corner is \(\sqrt{4^2+2^2}=\sqrt{20}\approx4.5\) inches from the center, less than 14.)
  3. The whole pan has area \(\pi\cdot14^2=196\pi\).
Answer. \(P=\dfrac{32}{196\pi}=\dfrac{8}{49\pi}\approx0.052\)

Probability density functions (problems 2, 3, 8, 9, 10)

2Find the endpoint that makes a pdf

Find \(A>0\) so that \(f(x)=\dfrac4\pi\cdot\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{(3+x)(3-x)}}\) is a pdf on \([0,A]\).
Solution
Plan. Total area must equal 1. First recognize \((3+x)(3-x)=9-x^2\), so the integral is an arcsine.
  1. \(f(x)=\dfrac4\pi\cdot\dfrac1{\sqrt{9-x^2}}\).
  2. \(\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{9-x^2}}=\arcsin\frac x3\).
  3. \[\int_0^Af\,dx=\frac4\pi\arcsin\frac A3=1\ \Rightarrow\ \arcsin\frac A3=\frac\pi4\]
  4. \(\dfrac A3=\sin\dfrac\pi4=\dfrac{\sqrt2}2\).
Answer. \(A=\dfrac{3\sqrt2}{2}\)

3Find k from a tail probability

\(\rho(x)=2xe^{-x^2}\) for \(x\ge0\). Find \(k>0\) with \(P(X>k)=\tfrac13\).
Solution
Plan. The tail probability is an improper integral. The substitution \(u=x^2\) does it.
  1. \[P(X>k)=\int_k^\infty2xe^{-x^2}dx=\lim_{t\to\infty}\Big[-e^{-x^2}\Big]_k^t=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(-e^{-t^2}+e^{-k^2}\right)=e^{-k^2}\] (The first term goes to \(0\).)
  2. Set \(e^{-k^2}=\tfrac13\), so \(e^{k^2}=3\).
  3. Take \(\ln\): \(k^2=\ln3\).
Answer. \(k=\sqrt{\ln3}\approx1.05\)

8Verify a function is a pdf

\(f(x)=\dfrac{3}{64}x\sqrt{16-x^2}\) for \(0\le x\le4\), and \(0\) otherwise. Verify that it is a pdf.
Solution
Plan. Two checks: \(f\ge0\), and total area equals 1.
  1. Nonnegative. On \([0,4]\), \(x\ge0\) and \(\sqrt{16-x^2}\ge0\), so \(f\ge0\). Elsewhere \(f=0\).
  2. Total area. Let \(u=16-x^2\), so \(du=-2x\,dx\) and \(x\,dx=-\tfrac12du\). Limits: \(x=0\to u=16\), \(x=4\to u=0\).
  3. \[\int_0^4\tfrac{3}{64}x\sqrt{16-x^2}\,dx=\tfrac{3}{64}\cdot\left(-\tfrac12\right)\int_{16}^0u^{1/2}du=\tfrac3{128}\int_0^{16}u^{1/2}du=\tfrac{3}{128}\cdot\tfrac23\cdot16^{3/2}=\tfrac1{64}\cdot64=1\]
Answer. both conditions hold, so \(f\) is a pdf.

9Find the upper endpoint

\(f(x)=\dfrac1{\sqrt x}\) for \(1\le x\le\alpha\), zero otherwise, is a pdf. Find \(\alpha\).
Solution
  1. \(\displaystyle\int_1^\alpha x^{-1/2}dx=\big[2\sqrt x\big]_1^\alpha=2\sqrt\alpha-2\).
  2. Set equal to 1: \(2\sqrt\alpha=3\), so \(\sqrt\alpha=\tfrac32\).
  3. Square: \(\alpha=\tfrac94\).
Answer. \(\alpha=\dfrac94\)

10A density defined by a differential equation

\(X\) has a pdf \(\rho(t)\) with \(\dfrac{d\rho}{dt}=-7\rho(t)\). Find \(P(X<7)\). (The PDF omits the solution.)
Solution
Plan. Solve the differential equation, then use "total area equals 1" to find the constant. The problem does not say where \(X\) lives, so the natural reading (exponential distribution) is \(t\ge0\).
  1. \(\dfrac{d\rho}{dt}=-7\rho\) has solutions \(\rho(t)=Ce^{-7t}\).
  2. Assume \(\rho\) is supported on \(t\ge0\). Total area: \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty Ce^{-7t}dt=\dfrac C7=1\), so \(C=7\) and \(\rho(t)=7e^{-7t}\).
  3. \[P(X<7)=\int_0^77e^{-7t}dt=\big[-e^{-7t}\big]_0^7=1-e^{-49}\]
Answer. \(P(X<7)=1-e^{-49}\) (essentially 1)
Watch out. the domain assumption (\(t\ge0\)) is what fixes \(C=7\). State it when you write this on a quiz.

Probability inside a solid (problem 12)

12A solid of revolution and a random point in it

Rotate the region between \(y=x\), the circle \(x^2+y^2=1\), and the x-axis (first quadrant) about the y-axis. (a) Use horizontal cross-sections to set up the volume. (b) A point is uniformly random in the solid. Set up the probability that its \(y\)-coordinate is greater than \(\tfrac12\). (c) What is the probability its \(y\)-coordinate is exactly \(\tfrac12\)? (The PDF omits this solution.)
Solution
Plan. Draw the region first. It is a slice of pie: below the line \(y=x\), inside the circle, above the x-axis. The line meets the circle where \(x=y=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\).
  1. Cross-section at height \(y\) (for \(0\le y\le\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\)): the region runs in \(x\) from the line, \(x=y\), to the circle, \(x=\sqrt{1-y^2}\). Rotating about the y-axis turns this into a washer with inner radius \(y\) and outer radius \(\sqrt{1-y^2}\).
  2. Washer area: \(\pi\left[(1-y^2)-y^2\right]=\pi(1-2y^2)\).
  3. (a) \[V=\int_0^{1/\sqrt2}\pi\left(1-2y^2\right)dy=\pi\left[y-\tfrac23y^3\right]_0^{1/\sqrt2}=\pi\left(\tfrac1{\sqrt2}-\tfrac1{3\sqrt2}\right)=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{3}\]
  4. (b) Probability \(=\dfrac{\text{volume above }y=\tfrac12}{\text{total volume}}\). \[P(y>\tfrac12)=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{1/2}^{1/\sqrt2}\pi(1-2y^2)\,dy}{\displaystyle\int_0^{1/\sqrt2}\pi(1-2y^2)\,dy}\]
  5. If you want the number: the top integral is \(\pi\left(\tfrac{\sqrt2}3-\tfrac5{12}\right)\) and the bottom is \(\tfrac{\pi\sqrt2}3\). \[P=1-\frac{5}{4\sqrt2}=1-\frac{5\sqrt2}{8}\approx0.116\]
  6. (c) A single value of \(y\) is a slice of zero thickness and zero volume.
Answer. (a) \(V=\dfrac{\pi\sqrt2}3\) (b) the ratio of integrals above, about \(0.116\) (c) \(0\)
