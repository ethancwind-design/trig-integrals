Lecture 44

From: Practice Problems + Solutions

Lecture 44: expected value (mean), median, and variance. Many answers in the PDF are printed with no work, and a few problems have the work only partly shown. Everything is written out here.

The formulas, and how to choose

  • Mean (expected value) of a density \(f\): \[\mu=E[X]=\int x\,f(x)\,dx\]
  • Variance: \[\sigma^2=V(X)=\int(x-\mu)^2f(x)\,dx=\underbrace{\int x^2f(x)\,dx}_{E[X^2]}-\mu^2\] The right-hand form is faster. Compute \(E[X]\), then \(E[X^2]\), then subtract. Standard deviation is \(\sigma=\sqrt{\sigma^2}\).
  • Median \(m\): the point with half the probability on each side, so \(\int_{\text{start}}^mf\,dx=\tfrac12\). Solve for \(m\).
  • Given \(P(X<x)\) instead of \(f\) (a cumulative probability): the density is its derivative, \(f(x)=\dfrac{d}{dx}P(X<x)\). Then compute the mean as usual. Check with the endpoints: \(P(X<\text{start})\) must be 0 and \(P(X<\text{end})\) must be 1.
  • Discrete random variable (finitely or countably many values): \(E[X]=\sum x\,P(X=x)\) and \(V(X)=\sum(x-\mu)^2P(X=x)\).
  • Find the constant first. If \(f\) has an unknown constant, make the total area 1 first. Everything else uses that value.

Problems

1Find c, then the mean

Find \(c\) so that \(f(x)=\tfrac12e^{-x}-ce^{-2x}\) is a pdf on \([0,\infty)\). What is the mean \(\mu\)?
Solution
Plan. The total area must be 1. Then compute \(\int xf\,dx\).
  1. \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\tfrac12e^{-x}dx=\tfrac12\) and \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty ce^{-2x}dx=\tfrac c2\).
  2. \(\tfrac12-\tfrac c2=1\), so \(c=-1\). The density is \(f(x)=\tfrac12e^{-x}+e^{-2x}\), which is positive.
  3. Mean: \(\mu=\displaystyle\int_0^\infty x\left(\tfrac12e^{-x}+e^{-2x}\right)dx=\tfrac12\int_0^\infty xe^{-x}dx+\int_0^\infty xe^{-2x}dx\).
  4. Useful facts: \(\int_0^\infty xe^{-x}dx=1\) and \(\int_0^\infty xe^{-2x}dx=\tfrac14\). (By parts: \(\int_0^\infty xe^{-ax}dx=\tfrac1{a^2}\).)
  5. \(\mu=\tfrac12\cdot1+\tfrac14\).
Answer. \(c=-1\) and \(\mu=\dfrac34\)

2A root density on [1, 2]

Find \(C\) so that \(f(x)=C\sqrt{x-1}\) is a pdf on \([1,2]\). Then find the mean.
Solution
  1. \(\displaystyle\int_1^2\sqrt{x-1}\,dx=\tfrac23(x-1)^{3/2}\Big|_1^2=\tfrac23\). So \(C\cdot\tfrac23=1\) and \(C=\tfrac32\).
  2. Mean: \(\mu=\tfrac32\displaystyle\int_1^2x\sqrt{x-1}\,dx\). Let \(u=x-1\), so \(x=u+1\). Limits \(0\) to \(1\).
  3. \(\displaystyle\int_0^1(u+1)u^{1/2}du=\int_0^1\left(u^{3/2}+u^{1/2}\right)du=\tfrac25+\tfrac23=\tfrac{16}{15}\).
  4. \(\mu=\tfrac32\cdot\tfrac{16}{15}=\tfrac{8}{5}\).
Answer. \(C=\dfrac32\) and \(\mu=\dfrac85\)

3Mean and variance of an arcsine density

\(X\) takes values in \((-1,1)\) with \(f(x)=\dfrac{1}{\pi\sqrt{1-x^2}}\). Find \(\mu\) and \(\sigma^2\).
Solution
  1. Mean. \(xf(x)\) is an odd function and the interval is symmetric, so \(\mu=0\).
  2. Variance. Since \(\mu=0\), \(\sigma^2=E[X^2]=\displaystyle\int_{-1}^1\frac{x^2}{\pi\sqrt{1-x^2}}dx\).
  3. Substitute \(x=\sin\theta\), \(dx=\cos\theta\,d\theta\), \(\sqrt{1-x^2}=\cos\theta\). Limits \(-\tfrac\pi2\) to \(\tfrac\pi2\). \[\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\sin^2\theta\,d\theta=\frac1\pi\cdot\frac\pi2\]
Answer. \(\mu=0\) and \(\sigma^2=\dfrac12\)

4Uniform distribution on [3, 5]

A real number is chosen uniformly between 3 and 5. Find \(\mu\) and \(\sigma^2\).
Solution
  1. The density is flat: \(f=\tfrac{1}{5-3}=\tfrac12\) on \([3,5]\).
  2. \(\mu=\displaystyle\int_3^5\tfrac x2dx=\left[\tfrac{x^2}{4}\right]_3^5=\tfrac{25-9}{4}=4\) (the midpoint, as expected).
  3. \(\sigma^2=\displaystyle\int_3^5\tfrac{(x-4)^2}{2}dx=\left[\tfrac{(x-4)^3}{6}\right]_3^5=\tfrac{1-(-1)}{6}=\tfrac13\).
Answer. \(\mu=4\) and \(\sigma^2=\dfrac13\)

Check: for a uniform distribution on an interval of length \(L\), \(\sigma^2=\dfrac{L^2}{12}=\dfrac{4}{12}=\dfrac13\).

5Variance of a parabola density

\(\rho(x)=\tfrac34\left(2x-x^2\right)\) on \([0,2]\). Find \(V(X)\).
Solution
Plan. Mean first, then \(E[X^2]\), then subtract.
  1. \(E[X]=\tfrac34\displaystyle\int_0^2\left(2x^2-x^3\right)dx=\tfrac34\left[\tfrac{2x^3}{3}-\tfrac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^2=\tfrac34\left(\tfrac{16}3-4\right)=\tfrac34\cdot\tfrac43=1\).
  2. \(E[X^2]=\tfrac34\displaystyle\int_0^2\left(2x^3-x^4\right)dx=\tfrac34\left[\tfrac{x^4}{2}-\tfrac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^2=\tfrac34\left(8-\tfrac{32}{5}\right)=\tfrac34\cdot\tfrac85=\tfrac65\).
  3. \(V(X)=E[X^2]-\mu^2=\tfrac65-1\).
Answer. \(V(X)=\dfrac15\)

6Find C from a tail probability, then decide if the mean is finite

\(X\) takes values in \((1,\infty)\) and \(P(X>a)=C\ln\dfrac{a^3+1}{a^3}\) for every \(a>1\). Find \(C\) and decide whether \(\mu\) is finite. (Hint: you can find \(C\) without any integral.)
Solution
  1. \(X\) is always greater than 1, so \(P(X>1)=1\). Put \(a=1\): \(1=C\ln\dfrac{2}{1}=C\ln2\). So \(C=\dfrac1{\ln2}\).
  2. The density is minus the derivative of the tail probability: \(f(a)=-\dfrac{d}{da}P(X>a)\). Write \(\ln\dfrac{a^3+1}{a^3}=\ln(a^3+1)-3\ln a\).
  3. \(f(a)=-\dfrac1{\ln2}\left(\dfrac{3a^2}{a^3+1}-\dfrac3a\right)=\dfrac{1}{\ln2}\cdot\dfrac{3(a^3+1)-3a^3}{a(a^3+1)}=\dfrac{3}{\ln2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{a(a^3+1)}\).
  4. Mean: \(\mu=\displaystyle\int_1^\infty a\,f(a)\,da=\dfrac3{\ln2}\int_1^\infty\dfrac{da}{a^3+1}\). For large \(a\) the integrand behaves like \(\dfrac1{a^3}\), and \(\int_1^\infty a^{-3}da\) converges. Since \(\dfrac1{a^3+1}<\dfrac1{a^3}\), comparison says our integral converges too.
Answer. \(C=\dfrac{1}{\ln2}\) and \(\mu\) is finite (about \(1.62\)).

7Mean and variance of f = 2x

\(f(x)=2x\) on \([0,1]\). Find \(\mu\) and \(\sigma^2\).
Solution
  1. \(\mu=\displaystyle\int_0^1x\cdot2x\,dx=\tfrac23\).
  2. \(E[X^2]=\displaystyle\int_0^1x^2\cdot2x\,dx=\tfrac12\).
  3. \(\sigma^2=\tfrac12-\left(\tfrac23\right)^2=\tfrac12-\tfrac49=\tfrac{9-8}{18}\).
Answer. \(\mu=\dfrac23\) and \(\sigma^2=\dfrac1{18}\)

8Mean and variance of f = 2(1 − x)

\(f(x)=2(1-x)\) on \([0,1]\). Find \(\mu\) and \(\sigma^2\).
Solution
  1. \(\mu=\displaystyle\int_0^1x\cdot2(1-x)dx=\left[x^2-\tfrac23x^3\right]_0^1=\tfrac13\).
  2. \(E[X^2]=\displaystyle\int_0^1x^2\cdot2(1-x)dx=\left[\tfrac23x^3-\tfrac12x^4\right]_0^1=\tfrac16\).
  3. \(\sigma^2=\tfrac16-\tfrac19=\tfrac{3-2}{18}\).
Answer. \(\mu=\dfrac13\) and \(\sigma^2=\dfrac1{18}\)

9Mean and median of f = 3x²

\(f(x)=3x^2\) on \([0,1]\). Find \(\mu\) and the median \(m\).
Solution
  1. \(\mu=\displaystyle\int_0^1x\cdot3x^2dx=\tfrac34\).
  2. \(P(X\le m)=\displaystyle\int_0^m3x^2dx=m^3\). Set equal to \(\tfrac12\): \(m^3=\tfrac12\).
Answer. \(\mu=\dfrac34\) and \(m=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{2}}\approx0.794\)

10Mean and median of f = 3(1 − x)²

\(f(x)=3(1-x)^2\) on \([0,1]\). Find \(\mu\) and \(m\).
Solution
  1. \(\mu=\displaystyle\int_0^13x(1-x)^2dx=\int_0^1\left(3x-6x^2+3x^3\right)dx=\tfrac32-2+\tfrac34=\tfrac14\).
  2. \(P(X\le m)=\displaystyle\int_0^m3(1-x)^2dx=\left[-(1-x)^3\right]_0^m=1-(1-m)^3\).
  3. Set equal to \(\tfrac12\): \((1-m)^3=\tfrac12\), so \(1-m=\dfrac1{\sqrt[3]2}\).
Answer. \(\mu=\dfrac14\) and \(m=1-\dfrac1{\sqrt[3]2}\approx0.206\)

11Mean and median of f = 4x³

\(f(x)=4x^3\) on \([0,1]\). Find \(\mu\) and \(m\).
Solution
  1. \(\mu=\displaystyle\int_0^1x\cdot4x^3dx=\tfrac45\).
  2. \(P(X\le m)=\displaystyle\int_0^m4x^3dx=m^4\). Set equal to \(\tfrac12\).
Answer. \(\mu=\dfrac45\) and \(m=\dfrac1{\sqrt[4]2}\approx0.841\)

When you are given P(X < x) instead of the density (problems 12 to 18)

Same three steps every time: (1) differentiate to get the density. (2) Write \(\mu=\int xf\,dx\). (3) Integrate (substitution or by parts).

12P(X < x) = (4/π) arctan x

Solution
  1. \(f(x)=\dfrac{d}{dx}\left[\dfrac4\pi\arctan x\right]=\dfrac4\pi\cdot\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}\).
  2. \(\mu=\dfrac4\pi\displaystyle\int_0^1\dfrac{x}{1+x^2}dx\). Let \(u=1+x^2\), \(du=2x\,dx\): \(\dfrac4\pi\cdot\dfrac12\ln(1+x^2)\Big|_0^1=\dfrac2\pi\ln2\).
Answer. \(\mu=\dfrac{2\ln2}{\pi}\approx0.441\)

13P(X < x) = ln(1 + x) / ln 2

Solution
  1. \(f(x)=\dfrac1{\ln2}\cdot\dfrac1{1+x}\).
  2. \(\mu=\dfrac1{\ln2}\displaystyle\int_0^1\dfrac{x}{1+x}dx\). Divide: \(\dfrac{x}{1+x}=1-\dfrac1{1+x}\).
  3. \(\displaystyle\int_0^1\left(1-\dfrac1{1+x}\right)dx=\big[x-\ln(1+x)\big]_0^1=1-\ln2\).
Answer. \(\mu=\dfrac{1-\ln2}{\ln2}=\dfrac1{\ln2}-1\approx0.443\)

14P(X < x) = ln(1 + x²) / ln 2

Solution
  1. \(f(x)=\dfrac{1}{\ln2}\cdot\dfrac{2x}{1+x^2}\).
  2. \(\mu=\dfrac2{\ln2}\displaystyle\int_0^1\dfrac{x^2}{1+x^2}dx\). Divide: \(\dfrac{x^2}{1+x^2}=1-\dfrac1{1+x^2}\).
  3. \(\displaystyle\int_0^1\left(1-\dfrac1{1+x^2}\right)dx=\big[x-\arctan x\big]_0^1=1-\dfrac\pi4\).
Answer. \(\mu=\dfrac{2}{\ln2}\left(1-\dfrac\pi4\right)\approx0.619\)

15P(X < x) = 2x / (1 + x)

Solution
  1. Differentiate with the quotient rule: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\dfrac{2x}{1+x}=\dfrac{2(1+x)-2x}{(1+x)^2}=\dfrac{2}{(1+x)^2}\). So \(f(x)=\dfrac2{(1+x)^2}\).
  2. \(\mu=2\displaystyle\int_0^1\dfrac{x}{(1+x)^2}dx\). Let \(u=1+x\), so \(x=u-1\). Limits \(1\) to \(2\).
  3. \(2\displaystyle\int_1^2\dfrac{u-1}{u^2}du=2\int_1^2\left(\dfrac1u-\dfrac1{u^2}\right)du=2\left[\ln u+\dfrac1u\right]_1^2=2\left[\left(\ln2+\tfrac12\right)-1\right]=2\ln2-1\).
Answer. \(\mu=2\ln2-1=\ln4-1\approx0.386\)

16P(X < x) = (4/3)(1 − 1/(1 + x)²)

Solution
  1. \(f(x)=\dfrac43\cdot\dfrac{2}{(1+x)^3}=\dfrac{8}{3(1+x)^3}\).
  2. \(\mu=\dfrac83\displaystyle\int_0^1\dfrac{x}{(1+x)^3}dx\). Let \(u=1+x\): \(\dfrac83\displaystyle\int_1^2\dfrac{u-1}{u^3}du=\dfrac83\int_1^2\left(u^{-2}-u^{-3}\right)du\).
  3. \(\displaystyle\int_1^2\left(u^{-2}-u^{-3}\right)du=\left[-\dfrac1u+\dfrac1{2u^2}\right]_1^2=\left(-\dfrac12+\dfrac18\right)-\left(-1+\dfrac12\right)=\dfrac18\).
  4. \(\mu=\dfrac83\cdot\dfrac18=\dfrac13\).
Answer. \(\mu=\dfrac13\)

17P(X < t) = 2^t − 1

\(X\) takes values between 0 and 1 and \(P(X<t)=2^t-1\). Find \(E[X]\). (This reads as \(2^t-1\). It is the only version that is \(0\) at \(t=0\) and \(1\) at \(t=1\).)
Solution
  1. \(f(t)=\dfrac{d}{dt}\left(2^t-1\right)=(\ln2)\,2^t\).
  2. \(E[X]=\displaystyle\int_0^1t\,(\ln2)\,2^tdt\). By parts: \(u=t\), \(dv=(\ln2)2^tdt\), so \(v=2^t\).
  3. \(\displaystyle\Big[t\,2^t\Big]_0^1-\int_0^12^tdt=2-\dfrac{2^t}{\ln2}\Big|_0^1=2-\dfrac{2-1}{\ln2}\).
Answer. \(E[X]=2-\dfrac1{\ln2}\approx0.557\)

18P(X < t) = sin(πt/2)

Solution
  1. \(f(t)=\dfrac\pi2\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi t}{2}\right)\).
  2. \(E[X]=\displaystyle\int_0^1t\cdot\dfrac\pi2\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi t}2\right)dt\). By parts: \(u=t\), \(dv=\dfrac\pi2\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi t}2\right)dt\), so \(v=\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi t}2\right)\).
  3. \(\displaystyle\Big[t\sin\tfrac{\pi t}2\Big]_0^1-\int_0^1\sin\tfrac{\pi t}2dt=1-\left[-\tfrac2\pi\cos\tfrac{\pi t}2\right]_0^1=1-\tfrac2\pi(0+1)\).
Answer. \(E[X]=1-\dfrac2\pi\approx0.363\)

Discrete random variables (problems 19 and 20)

19A random variable on the positive whole numbers

\(P(X=n)=\dfrac{12}{2\pi^2-9}\left(\dfrac1{n^2}-\dfrac1{n(n+2)}\right)\) for \(n=1,2,3,\ldots\) Find \(E[X]\).
Solution
Plan. \(E[X]=\sum nP(X=n)\). Multiplying by \(n\) makes the sum telescope.
  1. \(n\left(\dfrac1{n^2}-\dfrac1{n(n+2)}\right)=\dfrac1n-\dfrac1{n+2}\).
  2. \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\left(\dfrac1n-\dfrac1{n+2}\right)=\left(1-\tfrac13\right)+\left(\tfrac12-\tfrac14\right)+\left(\tfrac13-\tfrac15\right)+\left(\tfrac14-\tfrac16\right)+\cdots\) Every term cancels except the first two (\(1\) and \(\tfrac12\)): the sum is \(\tfrac32\).
  3. Multiply by the constant out front.
Answer. \(E[X]=\dfrac{12}{2\pi^2-9}\cdot\dfrac32=\dfrac{18}{2\pi^2-9}\approx1.68\)

20A die with alternating signs

A fair six-sided die has \((-1)^{n+1}(2n)\) dollars written on side \(n\). (a) What is the probability you do not lose money? (b) What is your expected earning? (c) Write an expression for the variance using summation notation.
Solution
  1. The values are \(n=1\): \(+2\), \(n=2\): \(-4\), \(n=3\): \(+6\), \(n=4\): \(-8\), \(n=5\): \(+10\), \(n=6\): \(-12\). Each has probability \(\tfrac16\).
  2. (a) Not losing money means a positive value: \(+2\), \(+6\), \(+10\). That is 3 of 6 sides.
  3. (b) \(E[X]=\tfrac16(2-4+6-8+10-12)=\tfrac{-6}{6}=-1\).
  4. (c) Variance \(=\sum(\text{value}-E[X])^2\cdot\text{probability}\). Since \(E[X]=-1\), value minus mean is value plus 1.
Answer. (a) \(\dfrac12\). (b) \(-1\) dollar. (c) \(\displaystyle V(X)=\sum_{n=1}^6\frac16\left((-1)^{n+1}(2n)+1\right)^2\), which works out to \(\dfrac{179}{3}\approx59.7\).
