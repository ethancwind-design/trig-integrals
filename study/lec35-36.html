Practice problems for Lectures 35 and 36: arc length and surface area of revolution. The PDF only prints final answers for problems 5 to 8 and leaves 22 unsolved, so those are worked out here too.

The idea in one picture

Zoom in on a tiny piece of a curve. It is almost a straight line, and that line is the hypotenuse of a tiny right triangle with legs \(dx\) and \(dy\). Pythagoras gives \(dL^2=dx^2+dy^2\). Everything in this unit comes from that. Pick whichever form matches how the curve is given:

  • Curve is \(y=f(x)\): \(\;dL=\sqrt{1+\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2}\,dx\)
  • Curve is \(x=g(y)\): \(\;dL=\sqrt{1+\left(\dfrac{dx}{dy}\right)^2}\,dy\)
  • Curve is parametric \(x(t),y(t)\): \(\;dL=\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{dx}{dt}\right)^2+\left(\dfrac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt\)

Arc length is \(\int dL\). Surface area is \(\int 2\pi\,r\,dL\), where \(r\) is the distance from the curve to the axis of rotation (a thin band is a cylinder of circumference \(2\pi r\) and height \(dL\)). About the x-axis, \(r=y\). About the y-axis, \(r=x\).

Arc length (problems 1 to 6)

1Curve defined by an integral

Find the arc length of \(y=\displaystyle\int_0^x\sqrt{e^{2t}-1}\,dt\) between \(x=0\) and \(x=1\).
Solution
Plan. You need \(y'\). The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus gives it without any integrating.
  1. \(y'=\sqrt{e^{2x}-1}\) (the integrand with \(t\) replaced by \(x\)).
  2. \(1+(y')^2=1+e^{2x}-1=e^{2x}\), so \(dL=\sqrt{e^{2x}}\,dx=e^x\,dx\).
  3. \[L=\int_0^1e^x\,dx=e^x\Big|_0^1=e-1\]
Answer. \(L=e-1\)

2Arc length of x to the 2/3

Find the arc length of \(y=\tfrac32x^{2/3}\) for \(0\le x\le3\sqrt3\).
Solution
Plan. Differentiate, square, add 1, then substitute.
  1. \(y'=\tfrac32\cdot\tfrac23x^{-1/3}=x^{-1/3}\), so \((y')^2=x^{-2/3}\).
  2. \(1+(y')^2=1+\dfrac{1}{x^{2/3}}=\dfrac{x^{2/3}+1}{x^{2/3}}\).
  3. Take the square root: \(\dfrac{\sqrt{x^{2/3}+1}}{x^{1/3}}\).
  4. \[L=\int_0^{3\sqrt3}\frac{\sqrt{x^{2/3}+1}}{x^{1/3}}\,dx\]
  5. Let \(u=x^{2/3}+1\). Then \(du=\tfrac23x^{-1/3}dx\), so \(x^{-1/3}dx=\tfrac32du\).
  6. Limits: \(x=0\to u=1\). \(x=3\sqrt3=3^{3/2}\to x^{2/3}=3\to u=4\).
  7. \[L=\tfrac32\int_1^4u^{1/2}du=\tfrac32\cdot\tfrac23\left[u^{3/2}\right]_1^4=8-1\]
Answer. \(L=7\)

3Parametric arc length (perfect square)

Find the arc length of \(x=t^2\), \(y=\tfrac13t^3-t\) between \(t=0\) and \(t=3\).
Solution
Plan. \(dL=\sqrt{(x')^2+(y')^2}\,dt\). Expect a perfect square.
  1. \(x'=2t\) and \(y'=t^2-1\).
  2. \((x')^2+(y')^2=4t^2+t^4-2t^2+1=t^4+2t^2+1=(t^2+1)^2\).
  3. \[L=\int_0^3(t^2+1)\,dt=\left[\tfrac{t^3}{3}+t\right]_0^3=9+3\]
Answer. \(L=12\)

4Arc length of cosh

Find the arc length of \(x=\cosh y\) between \(y=0\) and \(y=2\).
Solution
Plan. The curve is \(x=g(y)\), so integrate in \(y\). The identity \(1+\sinh^2y=\cosh^2y\) does the work.
  1. \(\dfrac{dx}{dy}=\sinh y\).
  2. \(dL=\sqrt{1+\sinh^2y}\,dy=\sqrt{\cosh^2y}\,dy=\cosh y\,dy\) (\(\cosh\) is always positive).
  3. \[L=\int_0^2\cosh y\,dy=\sinh y\Big|_0^2=\sinh2\]
Answer. \(L=\sinh 2\) (which equals \(\dfrac{e^2-e^{-2}}{2}\))

5Arc length with a perfect square in y

Compute the arc length of \(x=\tfrac14y^{-2}+\tfrac18y^4\) between \(y=1\) and \(y=2\).
Solution
Plan. The curve is \(x=g(y)\), so differentiate with respect to \(y\) and integrate in \(y\).
  1. \(x'=-\tfrac12y^{-3}+\tfrac12y^3\).
  2. Square. Middle term: \(2\cdot\left(-\tfrac12y^{-3}\right)\left(\tfrac12y^3\right)=-\tfrac12\). \[(x')^2=\tfrac14y^{-6}-\tfrac12+\tfrac14y^6\]
  3. Add 1: \(1+(x')^2=\tfrac14y^{-6}+\tfrac12+\tfrac14y^6=\left(\tfrac12y^{-3}+\tfrac12y^3\right)^2\).
  4. \[L=\int_1^2\left(\tfrac12y^{-3}+\tfrac12y^3\right)dy=\left[-\tfrac1{4y^2}+\tfrac{y^4}{8}\right]_1^2\]
  5. At 2: \(-\tfrac1{16}+2=\tfrac{31}{16}\). At 1: \(-\tfrac14+\tfrac18=-\tfrac18\).
  6. Subtract: \(\tfrac{31}{16}+\tfrac{2}{16}=\tfrac{33}{16}\).
Answer. \(L=\dfrac{33}{16}\)

6Arc length of ln|cos x|

Compute the arc length of \(y=\ln|\cos x|\) between \(x=0\) and \(x=\tfrac\pi4\).
Solution
  1. \(y'=-\tan x\), so \(1+(y')^2=1+\tan^2x=\sec^2x\) and \(dL=\sec x\,dx\).
  2. \[L=\int_0^{\pi/4}\sec x\,dx=\ln|\sec x+\tan x|\Big|_0^{\pi/4}\]
  3. At \(\tfrac\pi4\): \(\sec=\sqrt2\), \(\tan=1\), giving \(\ln(\sqrt2+1)\). At 0: \(\ln1=0\).
Answer. \(L=\ln\left(1+\sqrt2\right)\)

Surface area (problems 7 to 10)

7Parabola spun about the y-axis

The curve \(y=\dfrac{x^2}{8}\) between \(x=0\) and \(x=3\) is revolved around the y-axis. Find the surface area.
Solution
Plan. About the y-axis, radius \(=x\). The curve is \(y=f(x)\), so integrate in \(x\).
  1. \(y'=\dfrac x4\), so \(dL=\sqrt{1+\dfrac{x^2}{16}}\,dx=\dfrac14\sqrt{16+x^2}\,dx\).
  2. \[SA=2\pi\int_0^3x\cdot\tfrac14\sqrt{16+x^2}\,dx=\tfrac\pi2\int_0^3x\sqrt{16+x^2}\,dx\]
  3. Let \(u=16+x^2\), so \(x\,dx=\tfrac12du\). Limits: \(x=0\to16\), \(x=3\to25\).
  4. \[\tfrac\pi2\cdot\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac23\left[u^{3/2}\right]_{16}^{25}=\tfrac\pi6\,(125-64)\]
Answer. \(SA=\dfrac{61\pi}{6}\)

8Curve given as x of y, spun about the x-axis

The portion of \(3x=(y^2+2)^{3/2}\) between \(y=0\) and \(y=1\) is revolved around the x-axis. Find the surface area.
Solution
Plan. About the x-axis, radius \(=y\). The curve is \(x=g(y)\), so integrate in \(y\).
  1. \(x=\tfrac13(y^2+2)^{3/2}\), so \(x'=\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac32(y^2+2)^{1/2}\cdot2y=y\sqrt{y^2+2}\).
  2. \(1+(x')^2=1+y^2(y^2+2)=y^4+2y^2+1=(y^2+1)^2\), so \(dL=(y^2+1)\,dy\).
  3. \[SA=2\pi\int_0^1y(y^2+1)\,dy=2\pi\left[\tfrac{y^4}{4}+\tfrac{y^2}{2}\right]_0^1=2\pi\cdot\tfrac34\]
Answer. \(SA=\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\)

9Surface area of a sphere

Use calculus to compute the surface area of the sphere of radius 1.
Solution
Plan. A sphere is a half circle \(y=\sqrt{1-x^2}\) spun about the x-axis.
  1. \(y'=\dfrac{-x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\), so \(1+(y')^2=\dfrac{1}{1-x^2}\) and \(dL=\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\).
  2. Radius times \(dL\): \(\sqrt{1-x^2}\cdot\dfrac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}}=1\).
  3. \[SA=2\pi\int_{-1}^{1}1\,dx=2\pi\cdot2\]
Answer. \(SA=4\pi\)

10Set up only: x = ln y about the x-axis

Give an integral for the area of the surface generated by revolving \(x=\ln y\), \(1\le y\le2\), about the x-axis. Explain your answer.
Solution
Plan. Spun about the x-axis, so the radius is \(y\). The curve is given as \(x=g(y)\), so integrate in \(y\) with \(dL=\sqrt{1+(x')^2}\,dy\).
  1. \(x'=\dfrac1y\), so \(dL=\sqrt{1+\dfrac1{y^2}}\,dy\).
  2. Radius is \(y\), so \[SA=\int_1^22\pi\,y\sqrt{1+\frac1{y^2}}\,dy\]
  3. It simplifies: \(y\sqrt{1+\tfrac1{y^2}}=\sqrt{y^2+1}\) because \(y>0\).
Answer. \(SA=2\pi\displaystyle\int_1^2\sqrt{y^2+1}\,dy\)

Why: each thin piece of the curve spins into a band with circumference \(2\pi y\) (distance to the x-axis is \(y\)) and width \(dL\).

Parametric arc length, "factor out the pattern" (problems 11 to 15)

All five of these work the same way. \(x\) and \(y\) are powers of \(t\), so \((x')^2+(y')^2\) has a common factor of a power of \(t\). Pull it out of the square root, and the rest is a clean \(u\)-substitution.

11x = 3t^8, y = 2t^12

Find the arc length for \(0\le t\le1\).
Solution
  1. \(x'=24t^7\) and \(y'=24t^{11}\).
  2. \((x')^2+(y')^2=24^2t^{14}+24^2t^{22}=24^2t^{14}\left(1+t^8\right)\).
  3. Square root: \(24t^7\sqrt{1+t^8}\).
  4. Let \(u=1+t^8\), so \(du=8t^7dt\) and \(24t^7dt=3\,du\). Limits \(1\) to \(2\).
  5. \[L=3\int_1^2\sqrt u\,du=3\cdot\tfrac23\left[u^{3/2}\right]_1^2=2\left(2\sqrt2-1\right)\]
Answer. \(L=2\left(2\sqrt2-1\right)\)

12x = 3t^10, y = 2t^15

Find the arc length for \(0\le t\le1\).
Solution
  1. \(x'=30t^9\) and \(y'=30t^{14}\).
  2. \((x')^2+(y')^2=30^2t^{18}\left(1+t^{10}\right)\), square root \(30t^9\sqrt{1+t^{10}}\).
  3. Let \(u=1+t^{10}\), so \(du=10t^9dt\) and \(30t^9dt=3\,du\). Limits \(1\) to \(2\).
  4. \[L=3\int_1^2\sqrt u\,du=2\left(2\sqrt2-1\right)\]
Answer. \(L=2\left(2\sqrt2-1\right)\)

13x = 2t^6, y = 3t^4

Find the arc length for \(0\le t\le2\).
Solution
  1. \(x'=12t^5\) and \(y'=12t^3\).
  2. \((x')^2+(y')^2=12^2t^{10}+12^2t^6=12^2t^6\left(t^4+1\right)\), square root \(12t^3\sqrt{1+t^4}\).
  3. Let \(u=1+t^4\), so \(du=4t^3dt\) and \(12t^3dt=3\,du\). Limits: \(t=0\to1\), \(t=2\to17\).
  4. \[L=3\int_1^{17}\sqrt u\,du=2\left[u^{3/2}\right]_1^{17}\]
Answer. \(L=2\left(17\sqrt{17}-1\right)\)

14x = 2t^4, y = t^6

Find the arc length for \(0\le t\le1\).
Solution
  1. \(x'=8t^3\) and \(y'=6t^5\).
  2. \((x')^2+(y')^2=64t^6+36t^{10}=4t^6\left(16+9t^4\right)\), square root \(2t^3\sqrt{16+9t^4}\).
  3. Let \(u=16+9t^4\), so \(du=36t^3dt\) and \(2t^3dt=\tfrac1{18}du\). Limits: \(t=0\to16\), \(t=1\to25\).
  4. \[L=\tfrac1{18}\int_{16}^{25}\sqrt u\,du=\tfrac1{18}\cdot\tfrac23\left[u^{3/2}\right]_{16}^{25}=\tfrac1{27}(125-64)\]
Answer. \(L=\dfrac{61}{27}\)

15x = t^4, y = 2t^6

Find the arc length for \(0\le t\le1\).
Solution
  1. \(x'=4t^3\) and \(y'=12t^5\).
  2. \((x')^2+(y')^2=16t^6+144t^{10}=16t^6\left(1+9t^4\right)\), square root \(4t^3\sqrt{1+9t^4}\).
  3. Let \(u=1+9t^4\), so \(du=36t^3dt\) and \(4t^3dt=\tfrac19du\). Limits \(1\) to \(10\).
  4. \[L=\tfrac19\int_1^{10}\sqrt u\,du=\tfrac19\cdot\tfrac23\left[u^{3/2}\right]_1^{10}=\tfrac2{27}\left(10\sqrt{10}-1\right)\]
Answer. \(L=\dfrac{2}{27}\left(10\sqrt{10}-1\right)\)

Set up the integral (problems 16 to 21)

For these, follow the same four questions every time: (1) axis, so radius. (2) which variable and which \(dL\). (3) limits. (4) write \(\int 2\pi\,(\text{radius})\,dL\). Do not evaluate unless asked.

16Parametric, about the y-axis

Set up the surface area for \(x=2\sin3t\), \(y=\cos3t\), \(0\le t\le\tfrac\pi3\), revolved about the y-axis.
Solution
  1. Axis: y-axis, so radius \(=x=2\sin3t\).
  2. Parametric, so \(dL=\sqrt{(x')^2+(y')^2}\,dt\). \(x'=6\cos3t\) and \(y'=-3\sin3t\).
  3. \(dL=\sqrt{36\cos^2(3t)+9\sin^2(3t)}\,dt\).
Answer. \[SA=\int_0^{\pi/3}2\pi\,(2\sin3t)\sqrt{36\cos^2(3t)+9\sin^2(3t)}\,dt\]

17y = 9 − x², first quadrant, about the y-axis

Solution
  1. Axis: y-axis, so radius \(=x\). The curve is \(y=f(x)\), so integrate in \(x\).
  2. \(y'=-2x\), so \(dL=\sqrt{1+4x^2}\,dx\).
  3. First quadrant means from \(x=0\) to where the curve hits the x-axis: \(0=9-x^2\Rightarrow x=3\).
Answer. \[SA=\int_0^32\pi\,x\sqrt{1+4x^2}\,dx\]

18y = 4 − x², first quadrant, about the x-axis

Solution
  1. Axis: x-axis, so radius \(=y=4-x^2\). Integrate in \(x\).
  2. \(dL=\sqrt{1+4x^2}\,dx\).
  3. Limits: \(0\) to \(2\) (where \(4-x^2=0\)).
Answer. \[SA=\int_0^22\pi\left(4-x^2\right)\sqrt{1+4x^2}\,dx\]

19Parametric, about the x-axis

\(x=2t\), \(y=t^2\sqrt{1-t^2}\), \(0\le t\le1\), revolved about the x-axis.
Solution
  1. Axis: x-axis, so radius \(=y=t^2\sqrt{1-t^2}\).
  2. \(x'=2\). \(y'=2t\sqrt{1-t^2}+t^2\cdot\dfrac{-t}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}=2t\sqrt{1-t^2}-\dfrac{t^3}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}\).
Answer. \[SA=\int_0^12\pi\,t^2\sqrt{1-t^2}\,\sqrt{4+\left(2t\sqrt{1-t^2}-\frac{t^3}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}\right)^2}\,dt\]

20A circle piece, set up and evaluate

For \(y=\sqrt{4-x^2}\), for which \(x\) is it defined? It is rotated about the y-axis. Set up and evaluate the surface area.
Solution
  1. Defined when \(4-x^2\ge0\), so \(-2\le x\le2\).
  2. Spinning the left half gives the same surface as spinning the right half, so integrate \(0\le x\le2\).
  3. Radius \(=x\). \(y'=\dfrac{-x}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}\), so \(1+(y')^2=\dfrac{4}{4-x^2}\) and \(dL=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}\,dx\).
  4. \[SA=\int_0^22\pi x\cdot\frac{2}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}\,dx=\int_0^2\frac{4\pi x}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}\,dx\]
  5. Let \(u=4-x^2\), so \(du=-2x\,dx\). The integral becomes \(-2\pi\int u^{-1/2}du=-4\pi\sqrt u=-4\pi\sqrt{4-x^2}\).
  6. \[SA=-4\pi\sqrt{4-x^2}\Big|_0^2=0-(-8\pi)\]
Answer. \(SA=8\pi\) (a hemisphere of radius 2, area \(\tfrac12\cdot4\pi\cdot4\))

21x = e^t, y = e^(2t), about the x-axis

Solution
  1. Axis: x-axis, so radius \(=y=e^{2t}\).
  2. \(x'=e^t\) and \(y'=2e^{2t}\), so \(dL=\sqrt{e^{2t}+4e^{4t}}\,dt=e^t\sqrt{1+4e^{2t}}\,dt\).
Answer. \[SA=\int_0^32\pi\,e^{2t}\cdot e^t\sqrt{1+4e^{2t}}\,dt\]
Watch out. the radius is \(y\) (which is \(e^{2t}\)), not \(x\). The picture in your head should be the distance from the axis you spin around.

22Length of a curve defined by an integral

Find the length of \(y(x)=\displaystyle\int_1^x\sqrt{t^3-1}\,dt\) between \(x=9\) and \(x=16\).
Solution
Plan. Same trick as problem 1. The Fundamental Theorem gives \(y'\) directly.
  1. \(y'=\sqrt{x^3-1}\).
  2. \(1+(y')^2=1+x^3-1=x^3\), so \(dL=\sqrt{x^3}\,dx=x^{3/2}\,dx\).
  3. \[L=\int_9^{16}x^{3/2}\,dx=\tfrac25\left[x^{5/2}\right]_9^{16}\]
  4. \(16^{5/2}=4^5=1024\) and \(9^{5/2}=3^5=243\).
  5. \(\tfrac25(1024-243)=\tfrac25\cdot781\).
Answer. \(L=\dfrac{1562}{5}\)
