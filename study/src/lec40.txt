> Lecture 40: centroids and centers of mass. Several final answers in the PDF are printed with little or no work, so those are worked out here. Where my result disagrees with a number printed in the PDF I say so, and I checked those numerically.

## What to remember

- **Centroid** = center of balance of a region with constant density. **Center of mass** = the same thing when the density varies.
- "Average $y$-coordinate of the region" means $\bar y$, the $y$-coordinate of the centroid. (Problems 12 to 16.)
- For a region between $y=f(x)$ (top) and $y=g(x)$ (bottom) with density $\rho(x)$: $$m=\int\rho\,(f-g)\,dx,\quad\bar x=\frac1m\int x\,\rho\,(f-g)\,dx,\quad\bar y=\frac1m\cdot\frac12\int\rho\,(f^2-g^2)\,dx$$ For constant density, drop $\rho$ and $m$ becomes the area $A$.
- **Shortcuts, in order of how much work they save:**
- Symmetry about a vertical line: $\bar x$ is on that line. Symmetry about a horizontal line: $\bar y$ is on it.
- Quarter circle of radius $r$: centroid $\left(\tfrac{4r}{3\pi},\tfrac{4r}{3\pi}\right)$. Semicircle: $\tfrac{4r}{3\pi}$ above its flat edge.
- Triangle: average of the three vertices.
- Pieces with known centroids: weight each by its area (or mass).
- **Density questions** ("what density moves the center of mass left or right?"): put more weight on the side you want the balance point to move toward. Left-heavy density gives a smaller $\bar x$. Right-heavy gives a larger $\bar x$. A density symmetric about the region's line of symmetry leaves $\bar x$ unchanged.

## Problems

### 1. Which is the centroid of the quarter disk?
Q: Which is the centroid of $x^2+y^2\le1$, $x\ge0$, $y\ge0$? Options: (a) $\left(\tfrac4{3\pi},\tfrac4{3\pi}\right)$, (b) $\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac4{3\pi}\right)$, (c) $\left(\tfrac4{3\pi},\tfrac12\right)$, (d) $\left(\tfrac4{3\pi},\tfrac5{3\pi}\right)$, (e) $\left(\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right)$, (f) $\left(\tfrac5{3\pi},\tfrac4{3\pi}\right)$. Without calculating, is $\bar x$ more or less than $\tfrac12$?
Plan: Use symmetry first to cut the options, then the quarter-circle formula.
1. The quarter disk is symmetric about the line $y=x$, so $\bar x=\bar y$. That leaves only (a), (e).
2. Is $\bar x$ greater or less than $\tfrac12$? Look at the shape. Cut it by the line $x=\tfrac12$. The slices to the left of that line are taller than the slices to the right (the circle curves down as $x$ grows). More area is on the left, so the balance point is left of $\tfrac12$: $\bar x<\tfrac12$. That rules out (e).
3. Confirm with the integral: $A=\tfrac\pi4$ and $\displaystyle\int_0^1x\sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx=\tfrac13$, so $\bar x=\dfrac{1/3}{\pi/4}=\dfrac{4}{3\pi}\approx0.424$.
Answer: (a). $\bar x=\dfrac{4}{3\pi}\approx0.42<\tfrac12$.

### 2. y-coordinate of a centroid between a line and a parabola
Q: Find the y-coordinate of the centroid of the region bounded by $y=x$ and $y=x^2$.
1. They meet at $x=0$ and $x=1$. $x$ is on top.
2. $A=\displaystyle\int_0^1(x-x^2)dx=\tfrac12-\tfrac13=\tfrac16$.
3. $M_x=\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_0^1\left(x^2-x^4\right)dx=\tfrac12\left(\tfrac13-\tfrac15\right)=\tfrac1{15}$.
4. $\bar y=\dfrac{1/15}{1/6}=\dfrac{6}{15}$.
Answer: $\bar y=\dfrac25$

### 3. Centroid of a parabola region
Q: Find the centroid of the region bounded by $y=x^2$, $x=0$ and $y=1$.
1. The region is $0\le x\le1$, with top $y=1$ and bottom $y=x^2$.
2. $A=\displaystyle\int_0^1(1-x^2)dx=1-\tfrac13=\tfrac23$.
3. $M_y=\displaystyle\int_0^1x(1-x^2)dx=\tfrac12-\tfrac14=\tfrac14$, so $\bar x=\dfrac{1/4}{2/3}=\dfrac38$.
4. $M_x=\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_0^1(1-x^4)dx=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac45=\tfrac25$, so $\bar y=\dfrac{2/5}{2/3}=\dfrac35$.
Answer: $(\bar x,\bar y)=\left(\dfrac38,\dfrac35\right)$

### 4. Plate with density
Q: A plate is bounded above by $y=1$ and below by $y=2x^2-1$. The density is $\delta=1+x^2$. Find the center of mass.
1. Intersections: $2x^2-1=1\Rightarrow x=\pm1$. Region is symmetric about the y-axis and the density $1+x^2$ is too, so $\bar x=0$.
2. Mass: $(1+x^2)\left[1-(2x^2-1)\right]=(1+x^2)(2-2x^2)=2(1-x^4)$. $$m=\int_{-1}^1 2(1-x^4)\,dx=4\left(1-\tfrac15\right)=\tfrac{16}{5}$$
3. $1^2-(2x^2-1)^2=1-(4x^4-4x^2+1)=4x^2-4x^4=4x^2(1-x^2)$. Times $(1+x^2)$: $4x^2(1-x^4)=4x^2-4x^6$.
4. $$M_x=\tfrac12\int_{-1}^{1}\left(4x^2-4x^6\right)dx=\int_0^1\left(4x^2-4x^6\right)dx=\tfrac43-\tfrac47=\tfrac{16}{21}$$
5. $\bar y=\dfrac{16/21}{16/5}=\dfrac{5}{21}$.
Answer: $(\bar x,\bar y)=\left(0,\dfrac5{21}\right)$

### 5. Centroid under 2 plus root x
Q: A homogeneous object is the region between $f(x)=2+\sqrt x$ and the x-axis for $0\le x\le9$. Find its center of mass.
Plan: Homogeneous means constant density, so this is a centroid.
1. $A=\displaystyle\int_0^9\left(2+\sqrt x\right)dx=\left[2x+\tfrac23x^{3/2}\right]_0^9=18+18=36$.
2. $M_y=\displaystyle\int_0^9x\left(2+x^{1/2}\right)dx=\left[x^2+\tfrac25x^{5/2}\right]_0^9=81+\tfrac25\cdot243=81+\tfrac{486}5=\tfrac{891}{5}$.
3. $\bar x=\dfrac{891/5}{36}=\dfrac{891}{180}=\dfrac{99}{20}$.
4. $M_x=\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_0^9\left(2+\sqrt x\right)^2dx=\tfrac12\int_0^9\left(4+4\sqrt x+x\right)dx=\tfrac12\left[4x+\tfrac83x^{3/2}+\tfrac{x^2}2\right]_0^9=\tfrac12\left(36+72+\tfrac{81}2\right)=\tfrac{297}4$.
5. $\bar y=\dfrac{297/4}{36}=\dfrac{297}{144}=\dfrac{33}{16}$.
Answer: $(\bar x,\bar y)=\left(\dfrac{99}{20},\dfrac{33}{16}\right)=(4.95,\ 2.0625)$
Careful: $(2+\sqrt x)^2=4+4\sqrt x+x$. The middle term is the one people drop.

### 6. Center of mass with density 4 minus x squared
Q: Region bounded by $f=1$ and $g=x^2$, density $\delta=4-x^2$. Find the center of mass.
1. Intersections: $x^2=1$, so $x=\pm1$.
2. Mass: $$m=\int_{-1}^1(4-x^2)(1-x^2)\,dx=\int_{-1}^1\left(4-5x^2+x^4\right)dx=2\left(4-\tfrac53+\tfrac15\right)=\tfrac{76}{15}$$
3. $\bar x=0$: the integrand $x(4-x^2)(1-x^2)$ is an odd function, and the region is symmetric.
4. $f^2-g^2=1-x^4$. $(4-x^2)(1-x^4)=4-x^2-4x^4+x^6$. $$M_x=\tfrac12\int_{-1}^1\left(4-x^2-4x^4+x^6\right)dx=\int_0^1\left(4-x^2-4x^4+x^6\right)dx=4-\tfrac13-\tfrac45+\tfrac17=\tfrac{316}{105}$$
5. $\bar y=\dfrac{316/105}{76/15}=\dfrac{316}{105}\cdot\dfrac{15}{76}=\dfrac{79}{133}\approx0.594$.
Answer: $(\bar x,\bar y)=\left(0,\dfrac{79}{133}\right)$
Careful: the PDF prints $227/226$ for $\bar y$. That is above 1, which is impossible because the whole region sits below $y=1$, so do not copy it. The value above matches a numerical integration.

## Centroid plus a density question (problems 7 to 11)

Each has part (i) the centroid and part (ii) a density that moves $\bar x$ a certain way.

### 7. Region between y = x² + 2x and y = −x² − 2x, bounded by x = 1
(i) Find the centroid. (ii) Give a density that makes $\bar x$ smaller than the centroid's $\bar x$.
1. The curves cross at $x=0$ (set $x^2+2x=-x^2-2x$). The region is $0\le x\le1$, symmetric about $y=0$, so $\bar y=0$.
2. $A=\displaystyle\int_0^1\left(2x^2+4x\right)dx=\tfrac23+2=\tfrac83$.
3. $M_y=\displaystyle\int_0^1x\left(2x^2+4x\right)dx=\tfrac12+\tfrac43=\tfrac{11}{6}$, so $\bar x=\dfrac{11/6}{8/3}=\dfrac{11}{16}$.
4. (ii) To pull the balance point left, weight the left side more. For example $\rho(x)=1-x$ is largest at $x=0$ and shrinks to the right.
Answer: (i) $(\bar x,\bar y)=\left(\dfrac{11}{16},0\right)$. (ii) $\rho(x)=1-x$.

### 8. Region under y = 2|x| + 1 from −1 to 1
1. Symmetric about $x=0$, so $\bar x=0$.
2. $A=2\displaystyle\int_0^1(2x+1)dx=2\left[x^2+x\right]_0^1=4$.
3. $M_x=\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_{-1}^1(2|x|+1)^2dx=\displaystyle\int_0^1(2x+1)^2dx=\left[\tfrac{(2x+1)^3}{6}\right]_0^1=\tfrac{27-1}{6}=\tfrac{13}3$. So $\bar y=\dfrac{13/3}{4}=\dfrac{13}{12}$.
4. (ii) Any density symmetric about $x=0$ leaves $\bar x$ at 0. For example $\rho(x)=x^2$: each point at $x$ has an equally heavy mirror image at $-x$.
Answer: (i) $\left(0,\dfrac{13}{12}\right)$. (ii) $\rho(x)=x^2$.

### 9. Region between y = 2x + 1 and y = −2x − 1, bounded by x = 1
1. The lines cross where $2x+1=-2x-1$, at $x=-\tfrac12$. So the region is $-\tfrac12\le x\le1$, symmetric about $y=0$, and $\bar y=0$.
2. $A=\displaystyle\int_{-1/2}^1(4x+2)dx=\left[2x^2+2x\right]_{-1/2}^1=4-\left(\tfrac12-1\right)=\tfrac92$.
3. $M_y=\displaystyle\int_{-1/2}^1x(4x+2)dx=\left[\tfrac43x^3+x^2\right]_{-1/2}^1=\tfrac73-\left(-\tfrac16+\tfrac14\right)=\tfrac94$. So $\bar x=\dfrac{9/4}{9/2}=\dfrac12$.
4. (ii) Weight the right side more, for example $\rho(x)=x+1$, which grows with $x$ and is positive on the whole region.
Answer: (i) $\left(\dfrac12,0\right)$. (ii) $\rho(x)=x+1$.
Careful: the PDF suggests $\rho(x)=x$, but that is negative for $x<0$ and part of this region has $x<0$. A density can't be negative, so $x+1$ is safer.

### 10. Region under y = √10 x² from x = −2 to 2
1. Symmetric about $x=0$, so $\bar x=0$.
2. $A=2\displaystyle\int_0^2\sqrt{10}\,x^2dx=\tfrac{2\sqrt{10}}{3}\cdot8=\dfrac{16\sqrt{10}}3$.
3. $M_x=\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_{-2}^2\left(\sqrt{10}x^2\right)^2dx=\tfrac12\cdot10\cdot2\cdot\tfrac{2^5}{5}=64$.
4. $\bar y=\dfrac{64}{16\sqrt{10}/3}=\dfrac{12}{\sqrt{10}}\approx3.79$.
5. (ii) Weight the left more, for example $\rho(x)=2-x$ (largest at $x=-2$, zero at $x=2$).
Answer: (i) $\left(0,\dfrac{12}{\sqrt{10}}\right)$. (ii) $\rho(x)=2-x$.

### 11. Region under y = 5|x| from −1 to 1
1. Symmetric, so $\bar x=0$.
2. $A=2\displaystyle\int_0^15x\,dx=5$.
3. $M_x=\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_{-1}^1\left(5|x|\right)^2dx=\displaystyle\int_0^125x^2dx=\tfrac{25}{3}$. So $\bar y=\dfrac{25/3}{5}=\dfrac53$.
4. (ii) A symmetric density keeps $\bar x$ at $0$. For example $\rho(x)=x^2$.
Answer: (i) $\left(0,\dfrac53\right)$. (ii) $\rho(x)=x^2$.

## "Average y-coordinate" of a region (problems 12 to 16)

These ask for the centroid coordinate. Compute the area first, then the moment, then divide.

### 12. Between cos and sin from 0 to π/4
Q: Average $y$-coordinate of $0\le x\le\tfrac\pi4$, $\sin x\le y\le\cos x$.
1. $A=\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4}(\cos x-\sin x)dx=\big[\sin x+\cos x\big]_0^{\pi/4}=\sqrt2-1$.
2. $\bar y=\dfrac1A\cdot\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4}\left(\cos^2x-\sin^2x\right)dx=\dfrac{1}{2A}\int_0^{\pi/4}\cos2x\,dx=\dfrac{1}{2A}\cdot\tfrac12\sin2x\Big|_0^{\pi/4}=\dfrac1{4A}$.
Answer: $\bar y=\dfrac{1}{4(\sqrt2-1)}=\dfrac{\sqrt2+1}{4}\approx0.60$

### 13. Between sin and cos from π/4 to 3π/4
Q: Average $y$-coordinate of $\tfrac\pi4\le x\le\tfrac{3\pi}4$, $\cos x\le y\le\sin x$.
1. $A=\displaystyle\int_{\pi/4}^{3\pi/4}(\sin x-\cos x)dx=\big[-\cos x-\sin x\big]=0-(-\sqrt2)=\sqrt2$.
2. $\bar y=\dfrac1{2A}\displaystyle\int\left(\sin^2x-\cos^2x\right)dx=-\dfrac{1}{2A}\int\cos2x\,dx=-\dfrac1{4A}\sin2x\Big|_{\pi/4}^{3\pi/4}=-\dfrac{1}{4A}(-1-1)=\dfrac{1}{2A}$.
Answer: $\bar y=\dfrac1{2\sqrt2}=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{4}$ (the same region as Quiz 5, problem 12)

### 14. Under one arch of sine
Q: Average $y$-coordinate of $0\le x\le\pi$, $0\le y\le\sin x$.
1. $A=\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\sin x\,dx=2$.
2. $\bar y=\dfrac1A\cdot\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\sin^2x\,dx=\dfrac12\cdot\tfrac12\cdot\dfrac\pi2$ (since $\int_0^\pi\sin^2x\,dx=\tfrac\pi2$). That is $\dfrac{\pi/4}{2}$.
Answer: $\bar y=\dfrac\pi8$

### 15. Under cos from 0 to π
Q: Average $y$-coordinate of $0\le x\le\pi$, $0\le y\le\cos x$.
Plan: $\cos x$ is negative for $x>\tfrac\pi2$, so "$0\le y\le\cos x$" is empty there. The region is only $0\le x\le\tfrac\pi2$.
1. $A=\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos x\,dx=1$.
2. $\bar y=\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^2x\,dx=\tfrac12\cdot\dfrac\pi4$.
Answer: $\bar y=\dfrac\pi8$

### 16. Under ln x from 1 to 3
Q: Average $x$-coordinate of $1\le x\le3$, $0\le y\le\ln x$.
1. $A=\displaystyle\int_1^3\ln x\,dx=\big[x\ln x-x\big]_1^3=(3\ln3-3)-(0-1)=3\ln3-2$.
2. $M_y=\displaystyle\int_1^3x\ln x\,dx$. By parts, $u=\ln x$, $dv=x\,dx$: $\left[\tfrac{x^2}{2}\ln x-\tfrac{x^2}{4}\right]_1^3=\left(\tfrac92\ln3-\tfrac94\right)-\left(0-\tfrac14\right)=\tfrac92\ln3-2$.
3. $\bar x=\dfrac{M_y}{A}$.
Answer: $\bar x=\dfrac{\tfrac92\ln3-2}{3\ln3-2}\approx2.27$
Careful: $\int\ln x\,dx=x\ln x-x$, not $x\ln x$. The PDF's printed area $3(\ln3-1)$ leaves out the $-x$ part, so the answer there is different. The value above matches a numerical check.

## Comparing and set-ups

### 17. Greater, less, or equal
Q: The region is $(x-1)^2\le y\le2$. Circle greater than, less than or equal to and explain.
(a) The x-coordinate of the centroid vs the y-coordinate of the centroid.
(b) The y-coordinate of the centroid vs the y-coordinate of the center of mass with density $\rho=\pi$.
(c) The x-coordinate of the centroid vs the x-coordinate of the center of mass with density $\rho(x)=e^{-x^2}$.
1. **(a) Less than.** The parabola's vertex is at $x=1$ and the region is symmetric about $x=1$, so $\bar x=1$. For $\bar y$, more than half the region's area lies above the midway height $y=1$ (the region is wide at the top), so $\bar y>1$. (Exact value: $\bar y=\tfrac65$.)
2. **(b) Equal to.** A constant density ($\pi$ is just a number) cancels out of the center of mass formula, giving the centroid.
3. **(c) Greater than.** The density $e^{-x^2}$ is biggest near $x=0$ and shrinks as $x$ grows. The region runs from about $x=-0.41$ to $x=2.41$, so the left side gets more weight and the center of mass has $x<1$. The centroid's $x=1$ is greater.
Answer: (a) less than, (b) equal to, (c) greater than

## Solids of revolution (set up)

### 18. Solid between two parabolas, rotated about the x-axis
Q: The region between $y=(x-1)^2$ and $y=2-(x-1)^2$ is rotated about the x-axis. The density is $\rho(x)=x+1$. The center of mass is on the x-axis. Find its x-coordinate (set up is fine).
Plan: $\bar x=\dfrac{\int x\,dm}{\int dm}$ where each slice is a washer: mass $=\rho\cdot\pi\left(R^2-r^2\right)dx$.
1. Endpoints: $(x-1)^2=2-(x-1)^2\Rightarrow(x-1)^2=1\Rightarrow x=0$ or $x=2$.
2. On $[0,2]$ the top curve is $R=2-(x-1)^2$ and the bottom is $r=(x-1)^2$.
3. Washer mass: $(x+1)\,\pi\left[\left(2-(x-1)^2\right)^2-\left((x-1)^2\right)^2\right]dx$.
4. Simplify $R^2-r^2=(R-r)(R+r)=\left(2-2(x-1)^2\right)\cdot2=4\left(2x-x^2\right)$. (Because $1-(x-1)^2=2x-x^2$.)
Answer (set-up form): $$\bar x=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^2x(x+1)\,\pi\left[\left(2-(x-1)^2\right)^2-(x-1)^4\right]dx}{\displaystyle\int_0^2(x+1)\,\pi\left[\left(2-(x-1)^2\right)^2-(x-1)^4\right]dx}$$ Using the simplification above, this equals $\dfrac{\int_0^2x(x+1)(2x-x^2)\,dx}{\int_0^2(x+1)(2x-x^2)\,dx}=\dfrac{44/15}{8/3}=\dfrac{11}{10}$.
Careful: the radius is squared **inside** each term of $R^2-r^2$. The PDF's printed formula leaves one square off by mistake.

### 19. Solid from y = √x and y = √(1 − x), rotated about the x-axis
Q: The region bounded by $y=\sqrt x$, $y=\sqrt{1-x}$ and the x-axis is rotated about the x-axis. The density is $\rho(x)=x+2$. Find the x-coordinate of the center of mass (set-up is fine).
1. The curves cross where $\sqrt x=\sqrt{1-x}$, so $x=\tfrac12$. They hit the x-axis at $x=0$ and $x=1$.
2. The height of the region is $\sqrt x$ for $0\le x\le\tfrac12$ and $\sqrt{1-x}$ for $\tfrac12\le x\le1$.
3. Rotating gives discs of radius equal to the height, so disc area $=\pi x$ on the left piece and $\pi(1-x)$ on the right piece.
Answer: $$\bar x=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^{1/2}x(x+2)\,\pi x\,dx+\int_{1/2}^1x(x+2)\,\pi(1-x)\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_0^{1/2}(x+2)\,\pi x\,dx+\int_{1/2}^1(x+2)\,\pi(1-x)\,dx}$$ If you evaluate it: numerator $=\dfrac{31}{96}\pi$, denominator $=\dfrac58\pi$, so $\bar x=\dfrac{31}{60}\approx0.517$.
Careful: the switch from $\sqrt x$ to $\sqrt{1-x}$ happens at $x=\tfrac12$, so that is the middle limit in both pairs of integrals.

## Composite sheets

### 20. Two squares joined into a rectangle
Q: Two 2-inch square sheets weigh 80 g and 160 g, uniformly. They are joined along an edge into a rectangle, heavier square on the right. The rectangle has corners at $x=-1$ to $x=3$ and $y=2$ to $y=4$. Find the center of mass.
Plan: Treat each square as a point mass at its own center, then take the mass-weighted average.
1. Left square: $-1\le x\le1$, $2\le y\le4$. Center $(0,3)$. Mass $80$.
2. Right square: $1\le x\le3$, $2\le y\le4$. Center $(2,3)$. Mass $160$.
3. $\bar x=\dfrac{80\cdot0+160\cdot2}{80+160}=\dfrac{320}{240}=\dfrac43$.
4. $\bar y=\dfrac{80\cdot3+160\cdot3}{240}=3$.
Answer: $(\bar x,\bar y)=\left(\dfrac43,3\right)$
(The integral way gives the same answer with densities $20$ and $40$ grams per square inch.)

### 21. The cookies on a tray
This one depends on a picture (which grid points have cookies) and the PDF leaves the solution out, so there is nothing to solve from the text alone. The method is the same as problem 20: list each object's mass and center, then $\bar x=\dfrac{\sum m_ix_i}{\sum m_i}$ and $\bar y=\dfrac{\sum m_iy_i}{\sum m_i}$. The tray is a $14\times10$ rectangle with corners $(2,2)$ to $(16,12)$, so its mass of 1000 g sits at its center $(9,7)$.
