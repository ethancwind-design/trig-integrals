> Week 10 in-class practice: average value, center of mass and centroids. Solutions follow the answer key, with the algebra written out.

## Formulas you will need (from the top of the handout)

- Average value: $$f_{avg}=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^bf(x)\,dx$$
- Mass and moments of a region between $y=f(x)$ (top) and $y=g(x)$ (bottom), density $\rho(x)$: $$m=\int_a^b\rho\,(f-g)\,dx,\qquad M_y=\int_a^bx\,\rho\,(f-g)\,dx,\qquad M_x=\tfrac12\int_a^b\rho\,(f^2-g^2)\,dx$$ $$\bar x=\frac{M_y}{m},\qquad\bar y=\frac{M_x}{m}$$
- Uniform density (a plain centroid): take $\rho=1$, so $m$ is just the area $A$.
- For a rod (one dimension): $m=\int\rho\,dx$, $M_0=\int x\rho\,dx$, $\bar x=\dfrac{M_0}{m}$.
- Why $M_x$ has a $\tfrac12$ and $f^2$: a vertical strip of height $f-g$ balances at its midpoint height $\tfrac{f+g}{2}$. Its moment is $\tfrac{f+g}2\cdot(f-g)=\tfrac12(f^2-g^2)$.

## Part A: set up, don't evaluate

### A(a). Mass of a rod
Q: The mass of a rod on $0\le x\le3$ with density $\rho(x)=\sqrt{1+x}$.
Plan: Mass of a rod is the integral of its density.
1. $$m=\int_0^3\sqrt{1+x}\,dx$$
2. (Check, if you want to evaluate it.) $\tfrac23(1+x)^{3/2}\Big|_0^3=\tfrac23(8-1)=\tfrac{14}{3}$.
Answer: $m=\displaystyle\int_0^3\sqrt{1+x}\,dx=\dfrac{14}{3}$

### A(b). Moment M_x of a region
Q: The moment $M_x$ of the region between $y=\sqrt x$ and $y=x^2$ for $0\le x\le1$ (uniform density).
Plan: Decide which curve is on top, then use $M_x=\tfrac12\int(f^2-g^2)\,dx$ with $\rho=1$.
1. On $[0,1]$, $\sqrt x\ge x^2$, so $f=\sqrt x$ and $g=x^2$.
2. $f^2=x$ and $g^2=x^4$.
Answer: $$M_x=\tfrac12\int_0^1\left(x-x^4\right)dx=\tfrac12\left(\tfrac12-\tfrac15\right)=\dfrac{3}{20}$$
Careful: square the **whole** function: $(x^2)^2=x^4$, not $x^2$.

## Part B: average value

### 1. Average value, integration by parts
Q: Find the average value of $f(x)=xe^{-x}$ on $[0,2]$.
Plan: Integrate by parts, then divide by the interval length 2.
1. By parts: $u=x$, $dv=e^{-x}dx$, so $du=dx$ and $v=-e^{-x}$.
2. $$\int xe^{-x}dx=-xe^{-x}+\int e^{-x}dx=-xe^{-x}-e^{-x}$$
3. At 2: $-2e^{-2}-e^{-2}=-3e^{-2}$. At 0: $0-1=-1$.
4. Definite integral: $-3e^{-2}-(-1)=1-3e^{-2}$.
5. Divide by $b-a=2$.
Answer: $f_{avg}=\dfrac{1-3e^{-2}}{2}$

### 2. Mean Value Theorem for Integrals, sin squared
Q: Let $f(x)=\sin^2x$ on $[0,\pi]$. (a) Find the average value. (b) Find every $c$ in $(0,\pi)$ with $f(c)=f_{avg}$. (c) Explain why there is more than one.
Plan: Use the power-reducing identity $\sin^2x=\dfrac{1-\cos2x}{2}$ to integrate.
1. (a) $$\int_0^\pi\sin^2x\,dx=\int_0^\pi\frac{1-\cos2x}{2}dx=\left[\frac x2-\frac{\sin2x}{4}\right]_0^\pi=\frac\pi2$$
2. Divide by $\pi$: $f_{avg}=\dfrac{\pi/2}{\pi}=\dfrac12$.
3. (b) Solve $\sin^2c=\tfrac12$. Then $\sin c=\pm\dfrac{\sqrt2}2$. On $(0,\pi)$, $\sin c>0$, so $\sin c=\dfrac{\sqrt2}2$, giving $c=\dfrac\pi4$ or $c=\dfrac{3\pi}4$.
4. (c) The graph of $\sin^2x$ rises from 0 up to 1 at $x=\tfrac\pi2$ and falls back to 0. It is symmetric about $x=\tfrac\pi2$. The height $\tfrac12$ is crossed once on the way up and once on the way down, so there are two values. The theorem only promises **at least one**.
Answer: (a) $\tfrac12$. (b) $c=\dfrac\pi4$ and $c=\dfrac{3\pi}4$. (c) Two crossings, by the symmetry of the graph.

## Part C: center of mass and centroids

### 3. A rod heavier on one end
Q: A thin rod on $0\le x\le1$ has density $\rho(x)=\dfrac1{1+x^2}$. (a) Is $\bar x$ left or right of $\tfrac12$? (b) Find $m$, $M_0$ and $\bar x$.
Plan: For part (a), look at where the density is bigger. For (b), use the rod formulas.
1. (a) $\rho(0)=1$ and $\rho(1)=\tfrac12$. The density decreases from left to right, so the rod is heavier near $x=0$. The balance point is pulled left: $\bar x<\tfrac12$.
2. (b) Mass: $$m=\int_0^1\frac{dx}{1+x^2}=\arctan x\Big|_0^1=\frac\pi4$$
3. Moment: $$M_0=\int_0^1\frac{x\,dx}{1+x^2}$$ Let $u=1+x^2$, $du=2x\,dx$. Then $M_0=\tfrac12\ln(1+x^2)\Big|_0^1=\tfrac12\ln2$.
4. $\bar x=\dfrac{M_0}{m}=\dfrac{\tfrac12\ln2}{\pi/4}=\dfrac{2\ln2}{\pi}$.
5. Check against (a): $\dfrac{2(0.693)}{3.1416}\approx0.441<0.5$. Good.
Answer: $m=\dfrac\pi4$, $M_0=\dfrac{\ln2}{2}$, $\bar x=\dfrac{2\ln2}{\pi}$

### 4. Centroid under one arch of sine
Q: Find the centroid of the region under $y=\sin x$, $0\le x\le\pi$, above the x-axis. Use symmetry.
Plan: The arch is symmetric about $x=\tfrac\pi2$, so $\bar x=\tfrac\pi2$ for free. Then compute $A$ and $M_x$.
1. Symmetry: $\bar x=\dfrac\pi2$.
2. Area: $A=\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\sin x\,dx=[-\cos x]_0^\pi=1+1=2$.
3. Moment: $M_x=\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\sin^2x\,dx=\tfrac12\cdot\dfrac\pi2=\dfrac\pi4$ (using $\int_0^\pi\sin^2x\,dx=\tfrac\pi2$ from problem 2).
4. $\bar y=\dfrac{M_x}{A}=\dfrac{\pi/4}{2}=\dfrac\pi8$.
Answer: $(\bar x,\bar y)=\left(\dfrac\pi2,\dfrac\pi8\right)$

### 5. An arched window (no integrals)
Q: A window is a $4\times2$ rectangle ($-2\le x\le2$, $0\le y\le2$) topped by a semicircle of radius 2. Find the centroid.
Plan: Use the composite formula: weight each piece's centroid by its area. The semicircle's centroid is $\dfrac{4r}{3\pi}$ above its flat edge.
1. Symmetric about the y-axis, so $\bar x=0$.
2. Rectangle: area $4\cdot2=8$, centroid height $\bar y_1=1$.
3. Semicircle: area $\tfrac12\pi(2)^2=2\pi$. It sits on top of the rectangle, so its flat edge is at $y=2$. Its centroid height is $\bar y_2=2+\dfrac{4\cdot2}{3\pi}=2+\dfrac{8}{3\pi}$.
4. $$\bar y=\frac{8(1)+2\pi\left(2+\frac{8}{3\pi}\right)}{8+2\pi}=\frac{8+4\pi+\frac{16}{3}}{8+2\pi}=\frac{\frac{40}{3}+4\pi}{8+2\pi}$$
5. Multiply top and bottom by 3: $\dfrac{40+12\pi}{24+6\pi}$. Divide top and bottom by 2: $\dfrac{20+6\pi}{12+3\pi}$.
Answer: $(\bar x,\bar y)=\left(0,\ \dfrac{20+6\pi}{12+3\pi}\right)$
Careful: the semicircle's centroid is measured from its **own** flat edge. Add the 2 to place it on the window.

### 6. When density varies
Q: The lamina under $y=4-x^2$ for $0\le x\le2$ (above the x-axis) has density $\rho(x)=x$. (a) Find its mass and center of mass. (b) The uniform-density centroid is $\left(\tfrac34,\tfrac85\right)$. Compare and explain the shift.
Plan: Put $\rho(x)=x$ inside all three integrals. Here $f=4-x^2$, $g=0$.
1. Mass: $$m=\int_0^2x(4-x^2)\,dx=\left[2x^2-\tfrac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^2=8-4=4$$
2. $M_y$: $$M_y=\int_0^2x\cdot x(4-x^2)\,dx=\int_0^2(4x^2-x^4)\,dx=\left[\tfrac43x^3-\tfrac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^2=\tfrac{32}3-\tfrac{32}5=\tfrac{64}{15}$$
3. $M_x$: $$M_x=\tfrac12\int_0^2x(4-x^2)^2dx=\tfrac12\int_0^2\left(16x-8x^3+x^5\right)dx=\tfrac12\left[8x^2-2x^4+\tfrac{x^6}{6}\right]_0^2=\tfrac12\left(32-32+\tfrac{32}{3}\right)=\tfrac{16}{3}$$ (You can also substitute $u=4-x^2$.)
4. $\bar x=\dfrac{64/15}{4}=\dfrac{16}{15}$ and $\bar y=\dfrac{16/3}{4}=\dfrac43$.
5. (b) $\bar x$ moves **right**, from $\tfrac34$ to $\tfrac{16}{15}$, because the density $x$ puts more mass on the right side. $\bar y$ moves **down**, from $\tfrac85$ to $\tfrac43$, because the region is shorter on the right, so the extra mass there sits lower.
Answer: $m=4$, $(\bar x,\bar y)=\left(\dfrac{16}{15},\dfrac43\right)$

## Challenge

### 7. Parts, twice
Q: Find the centroid of the region bounded by $y=\ln x$, the x-axis and the line $x=e$.
Plan: The curve crosses the x-axis at $x=1$, so the region is $1\le x\le e$. Two integrals need integration by parts.
1. Area: $\displaystyle A=\int_1^e\ln x\,dx=\big[x\ln x-x\big]_1^e=(e-e)-(0-1)=1$.
2. $M_y=\displaystyle\int_1^ex\ln x\,dx$. By parts with $u=\ln x$, $dv=x\,dx$: $=\left[\tfrac{x^2}{2}\ln x-\tfrac{x^2}{4}\right]_1^e=\left(\tfrac{e^2}2-\tfrac{e^2}4\right)-\left(0-\tfrac14\right)=\dfrac{e^2+1}{4}$.
3. $M_x=\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_1^e(\ln x)^2dx$. By parts twice gives $\int(\ln x)^2dx=x(\ln x)^2-2x\ln x+2x$. At $e$: $e-2e+2e=e$. At 1: $2$. So $M_x=\tfrac12(e-2)$.
4. Since $A=1$, the coordinates equal the moments.
Answer: $(\bar x,\bar y)=\left(\dfrac{e^2+1}{4},\ \dfrac{e-2}{2}\right)$
